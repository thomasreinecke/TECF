ABSTRACT
Despite extensive research on the role of managers in digital transformation, there is limited
investigation into their readiness for organizational change. This knowledge gap is particularly
significant because managers' belief in digital readiness is a crucial prerequisite for effectively
guiding and implementing digital transformation initiatives. Therefore, this study aims to explore
the influence of managerial digital readiness on their intention to implement digital transformation.
To achieve this, we propose an integrated model that combines the digital readiness framework with
thetheoryofplannedbehaviour,consistingofthreesub-constructs:digitalstructurereadiness,digital
technology readiness, and digital strategy readiness. Our findings reveal that all these sub-constructs
positively impact managers' intention to implement digital transformation. This contributes to the
micro-foundations of digital transformation, specifically highlighting the pivotal role of managers.

larger economy (Vial, 2019). The incorporation of new digital technologies into the business model
has become an imperative for many firms competing in the global marketplace (Hanelt et al., 2021;
Verhoef et al., 2021). Incumbent firms are evolving by integrating digital technologies into their
business framework. This process is named digital transformation (DT)—“an organizational change

THEORETICAL BACKGROUND
The Definition of DT
DTisthereorganizationofthevaluepropositionandlogicofthefunctioningoftheorganization
by way of breaking down the traditional boundaries existing within the industries and providing
new value to the various stakeholders within the ecosystem (Hanelt et al., 2021; Li et al., 2018;
Wessel et al., 2021). This transformation signifies a profound shift that encompasses not only digital
technologies but also various other elements, including strategy, resources, capabilities, business
models, organizational structure, culture, workforce skills, and leadership (Alnuaimi et al., 2022;
Gong & Ribiere, 2021; Hanelt et al., 2021; Sousa-Zomer et al., 2020; Vial, 2019).
This definition is consistent with the increasing understanding in the research community that
a multilevel perspective is necessary for understanding the intricate nature of DT (Hanelt et al.,
2021; Verhoef et al., 2021; Vial, 2019). At the societal level, DT constitutes wider socio-economic
changes that influence and are influenced by technological advancements. For example, the rise of
digital channels has fostered customer orientation and interlinked markets (Chakraborty et al., 2024;
Hanelt et al., 2021). At the organizational level, DT involves the strategic use of digital technologies

The literature has shown that organizational culture, digital strategy, and agility and ambidexterity

The Role of Managers in the Process of DT
The effectiveness of an organization during the DT can be determined by the capabilities of the
manager. Digital leaders require essential skills such as a digital mindset, cognitive flexibility, the
cultivationofsocialcapital,digitalcapabilities,changemanagementexpertise,andmotivationalskills
(Li et al., 2018; Singh & Hess, 2017; Solberg et al., 2020). Empirical studies indicate that leaders’
digital skills are one of the major factors that enhance DT capabilities and overall organizational
performance (Malodia et al., 2023; Sousa-Zomer et al., 2020).
More significantly, numerous studies emphasize the significance of transformative leadership
in the context of DT. Transformational leaders are defined by their ability to engender trust, develop
leaders, partake in self-leadership and moral leadership, and encourage followers to pursue goals
beyond those that are immediate to the workplace context (Avolio, 1999; Bass & Avolio, 1994).
Such leadership is essential for organizations to continuously adapt and implement effective digital
strategies (Warner & Wager, 2019). Moreover, Alnuaimi et al. (2022) found that there existed a
positive relationship between DT and DT outcomes.
Executives are necessary for DT success. Leadership positions related to digital technology,
such as the CDO, can accelerate the transformation process by increasing awareness of disruptive
digitalthreatsandformulatinginnovativedigitalbusinessmodels(Berbel-Veraetal.,2022;Firketal.,
2021; Porfirio et al., 2021; Singh & Hess, 2017; Singh et al., 2020). Moreover, a CDO can provide
coordination benefits by initiating and designing organizational shifts, mediating political tensions,
negotiating with stakeholders, and assisting cross-functional collaboration during the process of DT
(Firk et al., 2021; Porfirio et al., 2021; Singh et al., 2020; Singh & Hess, 2017).

Any organization's journey towards DT depends heavily on its managers. However, this
transformation requires managers to be psychologically and behaviorally prepared to accept the
adjustments brought about by new technologies. This section seeks to introduce the concept of
managerial digital readiness.

DDR
Structural factors include the contextual conditions determining the manner in which an
organization approaches and changes its activities (Armenakis & Bedeian, 1999; Holt & Vardaman,
2013).Suchfactorsimplythatitiscriticalfororganizationstocreateappropriateclimates,structures,
and even tools that will allow their members to embrace change. In the readiness model proposed by

the importance of digital skills or knowledge at the individual level and incorporated perceived
organizational digital readiness and innovation barriers at the organizational level. This highlights
the discrepancy in DT scenarios.
However, their model does not reveal how individual-level structural factors are linked to
organizational-level structural factors. We believe that it is managers who use their personal digital
skills to form the organization’s support climate and facilitation strategies. Starbuck and Meyer

environments that effectively support the adoption of digital technologies. We conceptualize this
managerial propensity as DRR.

The relationship between technical readiness and acceptability of technological advancement in

DSR
Technological advancements have changed the dynamics of industries, requiring organizations
to respond strategically (Bharadwaj et al., 2013; Matt et al., 2015; Vial, 2019). The need for a
holistic digital strategy that aligns with both corporate and business strategies appears to be crucial
for achieving the goal of DT (Porfirio et al., 2021). The nature of DT involves more than just
information technology utilization; it also involves technology empowerment, the construction of
new organizational structures, and new value-creation approaches and business models (Gong &
Ribiere, 2021; Volberda et al., 2021; Warner & Wager, 2019). Formulating successful strategies
requires a shift in thinking from individual causal elements to interactions among them, including
distinct organizational capabilities within a firm (Park & Mithas, 2020).
Strategic readiness refers to an organization's comprehensive and systemic preparedness for
transformativechange(Holopainenetal.,2022).Itcoversaspectrumofmanagerialactivitiesdesigned

2022). Managers’ strategic readiness equips them with the knowledge necessary to articulate action
plans and establish guidelines for digital innovation and operations (Holopainen et al., 2022; Lokuge
et al., 2019). This dimension is also closely tied to the knowledge and cognition held by managers.

H1: Managers' digital structure readiness (DRR) has a positive influence on managers’ intention to

RESEARCH METHODOLOGY
This section describes how a thorough analysis of the pertinent literature served as a major
foundation for the development of the framework for the theoretical models of our quantitative
study. Structured questionnaires were used to collect the real data required for the study's goals. The
PLS-SEM method was used by the researchers to analyze both the inner and outer models at the
questionnaire-filled stage.
Data Collection

how it drives DT initiatives. China is now at the forefront of digital technologies uptake and use

Data Analysis
The PLS-SEM technique was realized using SmartPLS 3 software (Ringle et al., 2015) with
regard to the proposed research framework. The PLS-SEM approach is useful in addressing a
technical model with more than one dimension, numerous indicator variables, and many structural
pathways (Hair et al., 2019, 2021). As a significant advantage, the PLS-SEM approach is suitable for
research with a small sample size and where the data is not normally distributed (Chin, 1998; Hair
et al., 2017, 2022). This attribute is particularly pertinent in our context, where the respondent pool
is limited. Additionally, the PLS-SEM approach prioritizes predictive accuracy, aligning seamlessly
with our research objectives of exploring and elucidating relationships rather than solely validating
pre-existing theories.

DISCUSSION
Discussion of Findings
This research offers a novel viewpoint on the connection between managers’ INT to implement
DT and their level of digital readiness.
ThefindingofH1indicatesthatDRRislikelytoimprovemanager’sINTDT.Thisoutcomealigns
with the existing literature, which suggests that high levels of digital readiness among managers can
effectively cultivate an environment conducive to organizational change. Furthermore, this evidence
furthersupportstheassertionthatindividualreadinessforchangesignificantlyenhancesorganizational
readiness for change in the digital context (Vakola, 2013).
H2 also confirmed that managers’ desire to transform digitally in their organization is positively
influenced by their DTR level. While digital technology is not the sole factor in DT, managers with a
higher level of DTR tend to actively seek opportunities to utilize digital technologies (Gfrerer et al.,
2021;Jafari-Sadeghietal.,2021).Theadoptionofdigitaltechnologyinvolvesaprocesscharacterized
byexperimentationandexploration(McGrath,2010;Volberdaetal.,2021).HighDTRmanagersare
comparatively quick to observe and understand the issues and to accept new business models that
aresuitableforthedigitaleconomy.Theirpositiveorientationtowardsdigitaltechnologiestranslates
into a stronger willingness to initiate and execute DT within the organization.
Finally, the evidence supports H3 as it shows that managers’ DSR is positively associated with
INT DT. Managers with a high level of DSR are more likely to understand the strategic context
within which digital technologies are present and are able to devise and incorporate digital strategies
to create value (Bharadwaj et al., 2013). Furthermore, this finding highlights the notion that efforts
that yield strategic value (such as DT initiatives) are guided by organizational strategy. Therefore,

Theoretical Contributions
This study has considerable implications for the literature concerning DT from the managerial
point of view. First, our study enhances the appreciation of the aspects that managers relate with
respecttospearheadingafirm’seffortstowardsDT.Managers'perspectivesareimportantasDToften
involves substantial changes at the micro level of work (Vial, 2019). Most of the existing research on
managers in the context of DT is centered on the “traits” of managers, such as skills and knowledge
(Li et al., 2018; Sousa-Zomer et al., 2020), leadership (Alnuaimi et al., 2022), and functions (Firk
et al., 2021; Porfirio et al., 2021; Singh & Hess, 2017; Singh et al., 2020). However, due to the
dynamic characteristics of DT, the current literature offers limited focus on the psychology, beliefs,
and coping actions of the strategies of managers confronted with DT. This study addresses this issue
by focusing the examination of how managers’ digital readiness affects their INT DT. It enriches the
decision-making literature by describing how managerial digital readiness affects managers’ INT
to implement DT. This new perspective offers an enhanced comprehension of the decision-making
process within DT.
Second, the present work builds on the construct of digital readiness and proposes a
multi-dimensional managerial digital readiness construct. The existing literature on the digital
readiness model predominantly emphasizes the organizational level and is deficient in studies at
the individual level. Our expanded broadened perspective provides a beneficial framework for
analyzingmanagerialengagementinorganizationalchangeprocessesassociatedwithDTatthemicro
level (Armenakis & Fredenberger, 1997; Gfrerer et al., 2021; Holt & Vardaman, 2013). Moreover,
this research expands the body of knowledge by advancing the concepts with new measures that
operationalize three sub-dimensions of managerial readiness, extending the digital readiness model.
With the introduction of these factors, the study provides a finer and contextualized understanding
of the factors shaping managers’ intentions and behaviors in the particular area of DT (Huang et al.,
2023). This progress in conceptualization and measurement enhances the knowledge of prevailing
mechanisms within management through which decisions and actions are made in relation to DT in
organizations.
Third, our findings integrate the managerial digital readiness model and the TPB, which places
crucial focus on the importance of digital readiness on managerial decision-making. This integrated
framework highlights that managers' intentions are not exclusively constrained by the organizational
setting, but are also significantly determined by their beliefs, attitudes, and perceptions of control
regardingdigitalinitiatives(Ajzen,1991;Jafari-Sadeghietal.,2023).Bybridgingtheseframeworks,
we provide a comprehensive perspective that underscores how managerial belief and the response
to organizational change collectively enhance the managers’ INT to implement DT. This synthesis
enhances our knowledge of the relationship between individual and organizational characteristics.
In order to enhance the decision-making and implementation of digital strategies, it is important to
foster a supportive culture and equip the required digital skills.
Finally, by offering convincing proof that connects digital readiness to DT decision-making,
this study contributes to the empirical literature on managerial digital readiness. Earlier works on
the relationship between digital readiness and DT lack empirical validation (Gfrerer et al., 2021;
Pirola et al., 2019). Our quantitative findings demonstrate that managers are more likely to support
successfuldigitalinitiativesiftheyaremoredigitallyprepared.Thisempiricalvalidationenhancesthe
theoreticalunderstandingofdigitalreadinessandoffersconcreteinsightsforpractitioners,emphasizing
the need for targeted training and support programs that enhance managers' digital competencies.
By establishing these connections, our work establishes the groundwork for future studies that will
concentrate on managers' digital readiness in various organizational settings.

Practical Implications
DT presents significant challenges and opportunities for managers across industries. Based on
current research and best practices, several key implications were noted for managers as they prepare
their organizations for DT.
First, our findings highlight the necessity for managers establishing organizational structure and
environments that facilitate DT. As digital technologies reshape job roles, managers must prioritize
workforce development. This includes identifying skills gaps, providing training in emerging
technologies, and recruiting for digital talent. The emphasis should not be restricted to technical
skills only. A focus on both technical and soft skills is important. Managers need to actively cultivate
an organizational culture that embraces DT. This means that digital readiness should be fostered
throughout the hierarchy, risk-taking in terms of new ideas should be accepted, and employees
should be empowered to acquire and implement innovative solutions. They also need to diminish the
structuralboundariesandpromoteunderstandingacrossdifferentdepartmentsinordertoenhancethe
successfulimplementationofDTprojects.Furthermore,dedicatedbudgets,accesstodigitaltoolsand
platforms, and the provision of suitable training and assistance for staff members to actively engage
in DT initiatives are the necessary resources that managers should support.
Second, DTR among managers plays a significant role in successful DT. They should consider
their own digital education as one of the learning objectives. This constant dedication to learning is
essential in the rapidly evolving digital world. Managers should plan how to become knowledgeable
about relevant challenges that may affect their sector opportunities; seek out the various forms
of learning, both formal and informal, including online preparations, courses, workshops, and
peer-learningnetworks;andsetasidetimespecificallydedicatedtolearningaboutdigitalmovements
and their real-world applications. Moreover, they should also work on acquiring some specific
managerial abilities required in the era of digitalization. Improving digital communication skills,
including effective use of collaboration tools and social media platforms. Developing emotional
intelligence to address the human aspects of digital change and potential resistance. Developing the
ability to effectively lead virtual and distributed teams.
Third,managersmustarticulateacoherentvisionandroadmapforhowdigitaltechnologieswill
create value and competitive advantage. This requires assessing the organization's digital maturity,
identifyingstrategicpriorities,andaligningdigitalinitiativeswithoverallbusinessgoals(Bharadwaj
etal.,2013).Gainingsupportfromstakeholdersandfosteringaclimatethatisconducivetosuccessful
implementation depend on effectively communicating the strategic vision and the advantages of DT.
They should consider incorporating DT objectives and metrics into performance evaluations and
incentivesystems.ByrecognizingandrewardingemployeeswhoactivelycontributetoDTinitiatives,
managers can reinforce positive attitudes and behaviors towards DT. Moreover, they should explore
partnerships with technology vendors, start-ups, and academic institutions to access cutting-edge
capabilities and accelerate digital initiatives.
CONCLUSION
This study offers important insights into the significance of the managerial digital readiness in
ensuringthesuccessfulundertakingofDT.OurfindingsrevealthatmanagerialbeliefsinDRR,DTR,
andDSRpositivelyinfluenceINTDT.IntegratingthedigitalreadinessframeworkwithTPBprovides
an improvement of knowledge concerning the managerial-level constructs relevant to the process
of strategic and managerial decision-making. Through the analysis of 150 Chinese managers, the
study provides guidance for organizations to assess and develop the necessary digital competencies
among managers, enabling them to effectively navigate the complex challenges of digital disruption.
Overall, this research underscores the critical importance of fostering managerial digital readiness
for successful organizational transformation in the digital age.

limitation of this study. First, this study does not investigate potential variations in digital readiness

research could obtain larger samples drawn from diverse countries and cultural contexts. This would
allowforcross-culturalcomparisonsandpotentiallyrevealhowculturalfactorsinfluencemanagerial

longitudinalstudythatencompassesqualitativeapproachessuchasinterviewsorcasestudiescangive
an interesting perspective on how the issue of digital readiness changes over time as organizations
reach different stages of DT (Warner & Wager, 2019). Finally, while this study demonstrates the
positive correlation between managerial digital readiness and managers’ INT to implement DT, it is
unclear how this association is mediated. To support our claim, future studies could include various
digital readiness-related elements as mediating variables in the model.
