Developing dynamic capabilities
for successful digital transformation projects:
the mediating role of change management
Nizar Abdulwahab Ali Al‑Moaid1*
  and Senan Ghaleb Almarhdi1
Abstract
Digital transformation is a growth strategy that has attracted a significant amount
investment and is projected to boost the global economy by 2030. However, organiza-
tions face challenges in their efforts to develop effective approaches to digital transfor-
mation because of the constant need for new strategic choices. Dynamic capabilities
and change management are vital with respect to organizations’ability to maintain
competitiveness in the changing environment established by digital technologies.
The effective management of these changes is crucial, and failure is often the result
of the absence of a suitable change management model.This study examines the sig-
nificance of dynamic capabilities in digital transformation and explores the mediating
role of a well-defined approach to change management in the context of digital pro-
jects. A quantitative research approach is used to investigate a population of 902 man-
agers and team leaders working in the telecommunications sector inYemen. A ques-
tionnaire is developed and administered to a sample of 350 participants, and the data
collected were analyzed via SmartPLS.The findings reveal a positive relationship
between dynamic capabilities and the successful implementation of digital transforma-
tion initiatives. Effective change management techniques facilitate digital transforma-
tion by playing a mediating role in the abovementioned relationship.The results of this
research provide valuable insights into the theoretical framework underlying this topic
by integrating dynamic capabilities, change management, and digital transformation.
This information can help decision-makers in the telecommunications sector develop
a roadmap for successful digital transformation projects in their organizations.
Keywords: Digital transformation, Dynamic capabilities, Change management,
Telecom,Yemen

and satisfy emerging demands (Oh et al., 2022). This transformation is known as digi-
tal transformation (Pihir et al., 2018). Digital transformation enables organizations to
change their business models at lower costs. For example, Zoom, Skype and WhatsApp
are the most famous instant messaging applications, but they do not feature their own

shift from traditional telecommunications services (such as voice and short messages)
to over-the-top (OTT) applications (e.g., WhatsApp and Zoom), which occurred at a
late stage in this context, has been sufficient in urging telecommunications companies
to digitally transform their businesses (Valdez-de-Leon, 2016). For this purpose, lead-
ers must implement effective change management practices while improving their busi-

From a theoretical perspective, scholars such as Ellström et al. (2021), Rêgo et al.
(2021), Alkhamery et al. (2021), Marx et al. (2021), and Warner and Wäger (2019) have
explored the connection between dynamic capabilities and digital transformation. In
addition, scholars such as Bullock (2022), Hanelt et al. (2021), and Lugonja (2020) have
explored the link between change management and digital transformation. However,
few studies have addressed the notions of digital transformation, dynamic capabilities,
and change management jointly. This gap in the literature presents a significant opportu-
nity for this study to adopt various theoretical perspectives.
The scope of this study is limited to an examination of the impact of dynamic capabili-
ties on digital transformation and the mediating role of change management in Yemen’s
telecommunications sector. The unit of analysis on which this research focuses includes
every company in the telecommunications sector. Target respondents are leaders and
decision-makers who are responsible for digital transformation in the abovementioned
organizations and who work at the companies’ headquarters in Sana’a (the capital of
Yemen). Other factors that can impact digital transformation, such as wars, blockades,
inflation, and political conflicts, are beyond the scope of this study.
Theoretical background
Dynamic capabilities theory (Teece et al., 1997) and Kotter’s eight-step model (Kotter,
1995) are used as the theoretical foundation for this study as well as bases for construct-
ing the conceptual framework used in this study. Additionally, previous studies on this
topic are examined to identify the dimensions of the constructs included in the concep-
tual model, which are explained further in the subsequent sections.
Dynamic capabilities theory
On the basis of the resource-based view (Karimi & Walter, 2015; Mansoor & Yakoob,
2021), David Teece developed dynamic capabilities theory in 1997 (Teece et al., 1997).
This theory posits that managers should be able to integrate, build, and reconfigure both
internal and external competences to cope with the technological changes entailed by
the process of digital transformation (Karimi & Walter, 2015). According to Teece (2018),
these capabilities can be grouped into the following three main groups: sensing, seiz-
ing, and transforming. Sensing capabilities include activities that are related to scanning

and identifying technological opportunities in the organization’s external environment.
Seizing capabilities determine the time and resources needed to respond quickly to the
opportunities and threats sensed in this manner (Ellström et al., 2021). Transforming
capabilities are responsible for the integration of all organizational elements and the
alignment of those elements with the organization’s strategy (Warner & Wäger, 2019).
Scholars such as Ellström et al. (2021), Wang and Wang (2017), and Andreeva and Ritala
(2016) consider sensing, seizing, and reconfiguring capabilities representative of the
dimensions of dynamic capabilities. Moreover, Galetic and Vukelic (2016) add trans-
forming and integrating capabilities to this list. Additionally, Okano et al. (2021), Bogers
et al. (2019), and Warner and Wäger (2019) consider sensing, seizing, and transforming
to be dimensions of dynamic capabilities. On the basis of this theory and the literature
on dynamic capabilities, this study considers sensing, seizing, and transforming capabili-
ties to be the three dimensions of dynamic capabilities and thus to serve as the concep-
tual framework for this study.
Kotter’s Eight‑Step Model
Kotter’s model is a famous change management model that was initially developed by
John Kotter (Kotter, 1995). The model was derived from Kotter’s exploration of 100
organizations that underwent a change process. The model contains eight important
lessons that are converted into corresponding steps. Table 1 summarizes Kotter’s eight
steps.
Among a variety of change management models, Kotter’s model is best suited
to guide the process of digital transformation since it relies on a tested framework
for strategic transformation, which is the case for digital transformation (Auguste,
2013). In his book “Accelerate: Building Strategic Agility for a Faster-Moving
World”, Kotter employed the 8-step model to manage the changes associated with
what he called “The Big Opportunity”, i.e., changes that require accelerating agil-
ity. This need is present in the case of digital transformation. Kotter renamed the
same steps “Accelerators” (Kotter, 2014). Moreover, scholars such as Mickahail
(2015) and Kamau (2010) assert that highlighting the urgency of change with a
compelling message can motivate such change. Alkhamery et al. (2020a) claim that
a team with vision and a clear direction should lead such a change and influence
individuals. Additionally, scholars such as Hartl (2019), Kamuti (2013), and Kamau
(2010) agree that leadership should provide the capabilities needed to support and
empower change. Bullock (2022), Bobaker (2017), and Osibanjo et al. (2013) discuss
the treatment of change resistance and the fact that management should provide the
support necessary to make such changes permanent and sustainable. On the basis

Digital transformation
The digital transformation literature is scarce and focused on practice (Hyvönen, 2018).
The Scopus database contains no articles with "digital transformation" in the titles pub-
lished prior to 2000. Until 2016, the number of publications on this topic was excessively
low. The number of such articles subsequently increased, and 1371 publications were
recorded in 2019 (Al-Ali, 2020). Zaoui and Souissi (2020) note that previous studies
have provided a variety of definitions of digital transformation from different perspec-
tives. The above authors define digital transformation as a new development model that
reshapes the relationships among organizations, their stakeholders, and their clients by
offering new services or products. According to Oh et al. (2022), digital transformation is
the extension of the Fourth Industrial Revolution (Industry 4.0). Oh et al. (2022) assume
that digital transformation involves embracing digital technology to make social changes
through customer-centered business model improvements. Furthermore, Pihir et al.
(2018) define digital transformation in terms of the profound transformation of busi-
ness models, processes and competences with the goal of taking strategic advantage of
changes via digital technologies. On the basis of 23 papers, Al-Ali (2020) defines digital
transformation as an ongoing endeavor to exploit new technologies for the purposes of
business scope realignment, value creation and the facilitation of the internal operations
of an organization. This endeavor is implemented in response to the digital economy and
results in improved performance. Vial (2019) compiles definitions of digital transforma-
tion from 282 studies and concludes that digital transformation is a process that aims to
make significant changes in organizations and their properties on the basis of a combi-
nation of information, computing and connectivity. In reviews of the extant literature
on digital transformation, almost all scholars have identified technology as a pillar or
dimension of digital transformation. Scholars such as Oh et al. (2022), Saleh and Manju-
nath (2021), Nasiri et al. (2020), Singh et al. (2020), and Vial (2019) assume that the pro-
cess is an important dimension of digital transformation, while Udovita (2020), Gharib
(2019), Pihir et al. (2018), and Nowik (2018) claim that people are integral components
of digital transformation. Moreover, Mitroulis and Kitsios (2019), Hartl (2019), Hyvönen
(2018), and Westerman et al. (2014) consider digital transformation as representative of
a form of cultural change. Finally, Rêgo et al. (2021), Singh et al. (2020), Lugonja (2020),
and Tommasi (2018) agree that business model reinvention via new technologies rep-
resents the essence of digital transformation. In light of all the extant literature on this
topic, the authors have identified the following five dimensions of digital transformation:
technology, processes, people, culture and business model. The constructs contained in
these definitions and their dimensions are listed in Appendix I.

study develop a conceptual framework in line with the dimensions thus identified.

Hypotheses development
Three main hypotheses are proposed, each of which is divided into subhypotheses.
These hypotheses are based on the extant literature on this topic, in which context
the relationships among the study’s variables were examined and tested. The fol-
lowing sections elaborate on each hypothesis with reference to relevant previous
studies.
Firsthypothesis:impactofdynamiccapabilitiesondigitaltransformation
Studies conducted by Ellström et al. (2021), Rêgo et al. (2021), and Marx et al. (2021)
have revealed a relationship between dynamic capabilities and digital transforma-
tion. Alkhamery et al. (2021) investigate the role of dynamic capabilities in organi-
zations’ readiness for digital transformation. Additionally, Al-Ali (2020) highlights
key dynamic capabilities with respect to the formulation of a digital transformation
strategy. However, Warner and Wäger (2019) conclude that the building of digital
sensing capabilities, digital seizing capabilities and digital transforming capabilities
enables successful digital transformation processes. Alternatively, Karimi and Wal-
ter (2015) examine the role played by first-order dynamic capabilities in response to
digital disruption. Therefore, the authors develop the first hypothesis of this study as
follows:
H1: Dynamic capabilities have a positively significant impact on digital
transformation.
With respect to the first hypothesis, on the basis of the dimensions of dynamic
capabilities, the corresponding subhypotheses are as follows:
H1a. Sensing capabilities have a positively significant impact on digital transfor-
mation.
H1b. Seizing capabilities have a positively significant impact on digital transfor-
mation.
H1c. Transforming capabilities have a positively significant impact on digital
transformation.

Methodology
This study aimed to investigate the relationships among the variables and their corre-
sponding impacts. Therefore, a quantitative approach featuring analytical and deductive
methods was applied (Bougie & Sekaran, 2019; Nasiri, 2021). The unit of analysis was the
operator within the telecommunications sector in Yemen. According to Fry et al. (2017),
random sampling can be simple, clustered, systematic, or stratified. Stratified sampling

differs from cluster sampling. The former approach involves dividing a population into
subgroups known as strata. Each stratum shares a different value of interest. The sample
is then taken from each stratum via random sampling (Bougie & Sekaran, 2019). The
study included respondents from all operators in the Yemeni telecom sector who work at
different managerial levels and are greater than 900 in number. A random sample from
each stratum was selected to produce a total of 350 participants.
Data collection tools and analyses
Questionnaires help researchers collect large amounts of quantitative data. However,
they can lead to nonresponse results or errors (Creswell & Creswell, 2017). Question-
naires can be personally administered, mailed, or online. Each type of questionnaire
has unique advantages and disadvantages (Bougie & Sekaran, 2019). Questionnaires
(or surveys) provide quantitative data regarding the trends, opinions, and attitudes of
a research population sample (Creswell & Creswell, 2017). Accordingly, questionnaires
were used for data collection in this study. The questionnaire employed in this study is
divided into two parts. The first part contains questions concerning the demographic
information of respondents (e.g., age, gender, and qualifications). The second part con-
tains questions concerning the dimensions of the research variables. Each item rep-
resenting a particular dimension was scored on a 7-point Likert scale (ranging from
1=strongly disagree to 7=strongly agree). The questionnaire was designed to meas-
ure all dimensions of digital transformation as well as dynamic capabilities and change
management. The items included in the questionnaire were obtained from the extant
literature which has addressed similar variables or dimensions. The items and the corre-
sponding references are presented in Appendix II. The items were translated into Arabic.
A total of 17 academic professors reviewed the items by checking their language, valid-
ity, and consistency. We distributed questionnaires both on paper and via Google Forms.
The questionnaires were administered from October to November 2023. We targeted
respondents from all telecom operators in Yemen. We increased the number of paper
questionnaires to 400 to ensure that we obtained at least 350 successful responses to
help prevent any data loss.
Statistical methods
By 2015, the number of published articles using partial least squares structural equa-
tion modeling (PLS-SEM) had increased significantly compared with the number of
published articles using covariance-based structural equation modeling (CB-SEM) (Hair
et al., 2012). According to Sarstedt et al. (2021), the primary appeal of PLS-SEM is that
it enables researchers to estimate complex models that contain many constructs, indica-
tor variables, and structural paths without imposing distributional assumptions on the
data. More importantly, PLS-SEM is a causal predictive approach to structural equation
modeling (SEM) that emphasizes prediction in the process of estimating statistical mod-
els, the structure of which is designed to facilitate causal explanations (Sarstedt et al.,
2021). This technique overcomes the apparent dichotomy between explanations, which
have often been emphasized in academic research, and prediction, which serves as the
foundation for the development of managerial implications (Hair et al., 2019). Smart-
PLS is a user-friendly software package that requires little technical knowledge to use

enhance the accuracy of this research. Therefore, 335 responses were included in the
analysis, for a response rate of 97%.

DiscriminantvalidityusingtheHetertrait‑Monotrait(HTMT)approach
Discriminant validity refers to the extent to which a construct is empirically distinct
from other constructs included in the structural model (Henseler et al., 2015). Hair et al.
(2019) define the HTMT approach in terms of the mean item correlation across con-
structs (i.e., heterotrait-heteromethod correlation) relative to the average item correla-
tion for items measuring the same construct (i.e., monotrait-heteromethod correlation).
Discriminant validity problems are indicated if HTMT values are high (Hair et al., 2019).
The HTMT method reveals that all construct pairs are associated with values below
the thresholds of 0.85 and 0.90, as presented in Table 5. This threshold indicates that
the related constructs exhibit good discriminant validity. The correlations among the
constructs, which represent HTMT relationships, consistently demonstrate that the
constructs are distinct and do not overlap significantly. These findings collectively vali-
date the dependability of the measurement model, thereby supporting the claim that the
constructs used in this study capture unique and independent dimensions of the data
(Henseler et al., 2015).
Structural model assessment
When evaluating the results of PLS-SEM, it is first important to ensure the adequacy of
the measurement model. Once this adequacy has been established, attention can be paid
to the task of assessing the structural model. This assessment takes into account a range
of standard criteria, including the coefficient of determination ­ (R2
) (Shmueli et al., 2016).
The coefficient of determination ­ (R2
) metric quantifies the variance that is accounted
for by endogenous constructs within the model and thereby serves as an indicator of the
model’s explanatory capacity (Shmueli & Koppius, 2011). This metric is also commonly
known as a type of in-sample predictive power (Rigdon, 2012). ­R2
values range from 0

Discussion
Research Question 1 focuses on the impact of dynamic capabilities on digital transfor-
mation projects. Since H1 is supported, the findings of this study indicate a correlation
between an organization’s dynamic capabilities and its digital transformation initiatives
and projects. It is evident that dynamic capabilities have a noteworthy positive influence
on digital transformation within the telecommunications sector. The findings of this
study are congruent with those of previous studies, such as Ellström et al. (2021), Al-
Ali (2020), and Marx et al. (2021). The results of this study highlight the urgent need
for organizations to establish and maintain dynamic capabilities to ensure the ongo-
ing success of their digital transformation projects. Sensing capabilities pertaining to
new technologies and the ability to make timely decisions to seize and invest in those
technologies have been proven essential in a dynamic environment, such as the cur-
rent situation in the telecommunications industry according to Alkhamery et al. (2021).
Capabilities such as strategic agility and fast decision-making are enablers of success-
ful digital transformation according to Warner and Wäger (2019). Thus, this study high-
lights the importance of developing such capabilities to achieve success in the context
of digital transformation initiatives. Furthermore, the findings of this study reaffirm the
positive impact of dynamic capabilities on digital transformation.
With respect to Research Question 2, which focuses on the impact of change manage-
ment on digital transformation projects, H2 is also supported. These results prove that
change management has a significant influence on digital transformation. These findings
are consistent with those of previous studies, such as Hanelt et al. (2021) and Lugonja
(2020). Eliciting a sense of urgency and developing a guiding visionary for change have
been proven to have significant impacts on digital transformation according to Auguste
(2013). In contrast, the empowerment of those employees who implement the change
has not played an important role in digital transformation according to the results of
the testing of H2c. This finding may be explained by two factors. The first factor per-
tains to a lack of agility, in which context leaders are unable to decompose outcomes into
short-term milestones. The results of this study confirm the findings of scholars such as
Aldeen and Al-Hemyari (2024), Bullock (2022), Konopik et al. (2022), and Hartl (2019),

management in this context, it is clear that a structured change management model
mediates the relationship between dynamic capabilities and digital transformation
according to the results of the testing of H3. In other words, the implementation of
change management practices can be made more efficient when organizations possess
the dynamic capabilities necessary to support their digital transformation initiatives.
Specifically, leaders can elicit a sense of urgency for change by promoting the sensing of
new technologies among employees. The development of seizing and transforming capa-
bilities can support sustained change through activities that implement and anchor such
change. These findings are somewhat consistent with those reported by Bordeleau and
Felden (2019) who concludes that a structured step model and the continuous devel-
opment of dynamic capabilities are helpful with respect to managing digital transfor-
mation in a rapidly changing environment. Interestingly, team empowerment does not
significantly impact digital transformation in the assessments of the direct impact of
change management. However, team empowerment mediates the relationship between
the development of dynamic capabilities and digital transformation according to H3c.
Accordingly, even if staff empowerment does not affect the successful implementa-
tion of digital transformation projects directly, it explains how the development of the
organizational capabilities required to respond in a swift and flexible manner, such as
by providing information to employees at the right time and the capabilities necessary
to reconfigure resources and organizational skills, can greatly empower teams working
to support digital transformation according to Okano et al. (2021). Hence, the results of
this study not only substantiate the connections among these three constructs, as some
previous studies have suggested, but also explaining the mediating role played by change
management in this context, thereby making a notable contribution to the literature.
Conclusions
The main objective of this study is to explore the relationship between dynamic capabili-
ties and digital transformation while simultaneously investigating the mediating role of
change management in this relationship. A survey is designed and distributed to a ran-
dom sample of leaders in the Yemeni telecommunications sector, and the results reveal
a strong link between the dynamic capabilities of organizations and their digital trans-
formation efforts. Moreover, this study reveals that an organization’s ability to sense,
seize, and transform new technologies has positive impact on digital transformation
projects. Furthermore, digital transformation is driven by the organization’s business
model as well as its processes, people, culture, and technology. The authors use Kotter’s
eight-step model to identify the dimensions of change management. Although digital

transformation has been shown to be influenced directly by dynamic capabilities, it is
also impacted indirectly through change management practices. Accordingly, change
management can benefit from the acquisition of the dynamic capabilities required for
digital transformation projects. In summary, the development of organizational sensing,
seizing, and transforming capabilities has considerable impacts on digital transforma-
tion, both directly and indirectly via the influence of change management practices. The
findings of this research contribute from both theoretical and practical perspectives, as
discussed in the following sections.
Theoretical contributions
This study explores the role played by change management in the process by which
organizations acquire the dynamic capabilities necessary to support digital transforma-
tion endeavors. The study’s conceptual framework enriches the literature on the devel-
opment of the dynamic capabilities required for successful digital transformation. The
results of this research reaffirm the link between dynamic capabilities and successful
digital transformation efforts. Furthermore, this study verifies the significant positive
influence of dynamic capabilities on digital transformation projects in dynamic environ-
ments. This study also contributes to the extension of the change management model
to encompass digital transformation by combining Kotter’s eight-step model with the
notion of dynamic capabilities. Finally, this study explains how change management
mediates the relationship between dynamic capabilities and digital transformation,
which represents a notable contribution to the literature on this topic.
Practical contributions
This study highlights the urgent need for organizations (i.e., telecommunications opera-
tors) to develop and maintain dynamic capabilities to ensure the success of digital trans-
formation projects. Consequently, the findings of this study can be especially beneficial
for organizations operating in the telecommunications industry as they embark on their
digital transformation journeys. For example, leaders in these organizations should focus
on the tasks of cultivating and developing sensing capabilities through their research
and development (R&D) units. These tasks involve monitoring emerging technolo-
gies and assessing the digital efforts of competitors in the market. Additionally, lead-
ers should possess the ability to secure funding for new digital investments and make
swift decisions regarding the selection and implementation of appropriate digital solu-
tions. Furthermore, leaders must possess the ability to transform and reconfigure the
technologies thus acquired to add value and obtain competitive advantages. To a large
extent, the results of this study can encourage leaders to exhibit agility in the context of
digital transformation projects by pursuing achievable short-term outcomes. Moreover,
the proposed approach to change management can help leaders overcome the resistance
to change associated with new processes or business models.
Future research directions
This study features several limitations that can be transformed into recommendations
for future researchers working in the same domain. As digital transformation has a
significant effect on organizational culture (Hartl, 2019; Hyvönen, 2018), it would be

beneficial to target all employees, i.e., not merely decision-makers, to broaden the results
of this study. Furthermore, targeting respondents in another country would account for
more cultural differences in the results. Additionally, the authors consider the telecom-
munications sector to be the representative of digital service providers, but other types
of digital service providers, such as banks and mobile money companies, also offer digi-
tal financial services. Thus, the incorporation of these industries into future researches
would be beneficial. Moreover, a wide range of change management models have not
been discussed in this context, and it is possible that some of these models could be
applicable to the case of digital transformation. Future researchers could explore addi-

relies on a quantitative method, the incorporation of qualitative approach in the same

ful digital transformation.
