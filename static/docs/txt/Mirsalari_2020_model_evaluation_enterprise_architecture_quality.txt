A B S T R A C T
Today, most organizations use an enterprise architecture (EA) approach as a tool to increase the power of
management on the organization's information technology. Enterprise architecture is a set of processes that helps
an organization to translate its vision into an eﬀective change in the organization's scope by providing a clear
understanding of its current state. The purpose of this research is to identify EA quality attributes and its eva-
luation indicators in the organization. This study was conducted by using mixed method, including qualitative
and quantitative parts. In the qualitative section, a variety of EA evaluation indicators were identiﬁed by a
systematic literature review (SLR) approach, then in the quantitative section the survey data were collected by a
questionnaire prepared based on the qualitative part and then exploratory factor analysis (EFA) and con-
ﬁrmatory factor analysis (CFA) were performed. This research presents an EA evaluation model that has seven
main quality attributes including alignment and integrity, quality of EA products and services, security, main-
tainability and portability, reliability, reusability and scalability, and 30 indicators that address all aspects of
enterprise architecture. Through this model, organizations can evaluate the quality of implemented EA or AS-IS
status of EA and take steps to improve it.
1. Introductions
Nowadays, the importance of organizational ﬂexibility against ex-
ternal pressures, such as change in business, mission and organizational
structure, rapid technological changes and the distribution of organi-
zations in the geographic scope is inevitable. Enterprise architecture
(EA) not only aﬀects the internal resources and components of the or-
ganization, such as IT elements, processes, or even employees (Anaya
and Ortiz, 2005; Rood, 1994; Ross et al., 2006; Zachman, 1997); but
also oversees outsourcing (Alaeddini and Salekfard, 2013; Hope, 2015).
A high-level enterprise architecture (meta-architecture) includes in-
formation technology systems of enterprise (including hardware and
software) and their relationships, and their related processes, functions,
groups, and individuals. From a functional view, an enterprise archi-
tecture describes how the elements of the information technology can
work together as a whole, with groups and individuals in the organi-
zation (Buchanan, 2001; Tamm, Seddon, Shanks, & Reynolds, 2011).
Indeed, EA is a holistic and integrated viewpoint from all aspects of the
organization's business (Carvalho & Sousa, 2014; Gong & Janssen,
2019;), hence many organizations have invested in this area in order to
comprehend and realize their business or are allocating resources and
investing in EA projects (Tamm et al., 2011). Therefore, it is required to
have indicators and identiﬁcation factors to recognize and evaluate
enterprise architecture for reducing the risk of the EA implementation
projects and preventing the waste of ﬁnancial and time resources
(Bernus et al., 2016; Gong & Janssen, 2019). Another considerable
reason for evaluating EA is to have a clear understanding of EA value
that is related to the required communication for aligning diﬀerent
stakeholders. A positive perception of EA value is very important to
ensure the continuous commitment of EA stakeholders (Foorthuis, Van
Steenbergen, Brinkkemper, & Bruls, 2016; Gong & Janssen, 2019;
Robertson, Peko, & Sundaram, 2018; Van der Raadt & Bonnet, 2010).
Evaluation of enterprise architecture is deﬁned as the process of de-
termining the beneﬁts, feasibility and value of EA artifacts (Österlind
et al., 2013). EA evaluation has grown into an important topic for en-
terprises that need to organize large portfolios of their projects and
adapt IT to their business processes over time (Neimi & Pekkola, 2013).
Evaluation leads to engender information that assists with making
judgments and decisions on a program, service, policy or ISs integra-
tion, and then guiding decision makers to take practical actions
(Nikpay, Ahmad, & Kia, 2017).

1.1. Motivation for this research
Evaluation generates qualitative (descriptive) and quantitative
(numeric) information. Generating quantitative information by using
metrics is commonly referred to as measurement (Hämäläinen, 2008).
Descriptive and numeric information can be used to complement each
other. The problem of evaluating EA has been extensively considered in
the literature. Up to now several eﬀorts in EA research have targeted
the identiﬁcation of EA indices; and numerous enterprise architecture
evaluation models and methods have been proposed (Buchanan, 2001;
Gammelgård, Ekstedt, & Närman, 2007; Gunther, 2014; Khayami,
2011; Klein & Gagliardi, 2010; Lakhrouit, Baina, & Benali, 2014; Neimi
& Pekkola, 2013; Nikpay, Ahmad, Kia et al., 2017). But there are some
defects, some methods have just focused on evaluating an implemented
EA (Buchanan, 2001; Morganwalp & Sage, 2011; Nikpay, Ahmad, Kia
et al., 2017) or the most of the proposed measures are qualitative
(Gammelgård et al., 2007; Lakhrouit et al., 2014; Neimi & Pekkola,
2013). The researchers generally acknowledged the diﬃculty of asses-
sing the impact of EA (Gunther, 2014). Moreover, an important point to
be noted is that, given that an enterprise architecture is associated with
four layers including business, information, application, and infra-
structure layers (Klein & Gagliardi, 2010; Nikpay, Ahmad, Rouhani, &
Mahrin, 2017; Razavi & Aliee, 2009; Rouhani, Mahrin, Nikpay, Nikfard,
& Najafabadi, 2015), so an appropriate evaluation requires a compre-
hensive evaluation, which addresses all layers of EA. However, a great
majority of the existing EA evaluation models are related to the appli-
cation and informationlayers and a tiny minority of them consider the
business and infrastructure layers (Bernard, 2012; Gammelgård et al.,
2007; Giachetti, 2012; Zandi & Tavana, 2012). Also, some of them are
attained by means of multiple practical EA implementation projects and
there is no appropriate theoretical and scientiﬁc foundation behind
them (Nikpay, Ahmad, Kia et al., 2017). Evaluation and measurement
concepts and practices are advanced in diverse domains (e.g. program
management, software engineering, quality management). Evaluation
and measurement mean diﬀerent things to diﬀerent people in diﬀerent
contexts. It appears that there is no general evaluation theory
(Hämäläinen, 2008). "Regular evaluation of the eﬀectiveness of the
enterprise architecture program is crucial to understand the net eﬀect
of the varied and complex architecture process on the environment and
to provide recommendations for continuous improvement (Leganza,
2003)". As a result, the aim of this research is to recognize EA quality
indicators in order to measure all layers of EA and provide a model for
EA quality evaluation in the organization.
1.2. Study goals and contributions
The main objective of this study is to develop an EA evaluation
model for analyzing EA that facilitates the rational decision making
about information systems and enterprise architecture in an organiza-
tion. This research undertook two sections accordingly: qualitative and
quantitative sections. The ﬁrst section provides the foundation (EA
quality indicators) through a systematic literature review (SLR) and
interviews with EA experts. The second section focuses on developing a
model for evaluating EA implementation based on the foundation and
information from SLR, data analyzing, and EA experts’ points of view.
In fact, in the qualitative part, EA evaluation indicators have been
identiﬁed through literature review and then classiﬁed, second a
quantitative analysis has been done using the statistical analysis
methods in order to create a validated model for EA evaluation.
1.3. Structure of the paper
The remainder of this paper is divided into the following parts: re-
lated works are described in Section 2; the research methodology is
presented in Section 3; the proposed EA evaluation model, discussion
and also future research are given in Sections 4 and 5. Finally the
conclusions from this study are expressed in Section 6.
2. Related works
"Enterprise architecture is the representation of the structure and
behavior of an enterprise’s IT landscape in relation to its business en-
vironment. It reﬂects the current and future use of IT in the enterprise
and provides a roadmap to reach a future state (Bente, Bombosch, &
Langade, 2012)". Yet, there is no single agreed deﬁnition of EA ac-
cording to the literature review (Robertson et al., 2018) and all of EA
research approve that EA is an integrated and holistic vision of the
enterprise’s IT resources that is embodied in its elements (people,
processes, applications, and so on) and their relationships to each other
and the environment, and also the principles guiding its design and
evolution (Carvalho & Sousa, 2014; Fallmyr & Bygstad, 2014; Gong &
Janssen, 2019; Ross, Weill, & Robertson, 2006; Saat, Winter, Franke,
Lagerstroem, & Ekstedt, 2011; Shah & ElKourdi, 2007; Zachman, 1997).
Currently, many organizations are dynamically developing their EA
processes that indicates the importance of the EA evaluation concept in
practice. In addition, it does not seem to be completely clear how to
evaluate EA. Describing and evaluating EA, be it quantitatively or
qualitatively, can be challenging and this diﬃculty is recognized by
both practitioners and researchers, however, many eﬀorts have been
made in this regard. According to the literature, EA evaluation usually
deals with several diﬀerent approaches and a variety of methods are
being developed for evaluation of EA programs (Hämäläinen, 2008),
For example, the EA maturity models (Lakhrouit et al., 2014; Van der
Raadt & Bonnet, 2010; Ylimäki, 2006, 2007), EA quality attributes
(Gammelgård et al., 2007; Khayami, 2011; Klein & Gagliardi, 2010;
Närman, Johnson, & Nordstrom, 2007; Neimi & Pekkola, 2013), EA
eﬀectiveness assessment (Buchanan, 2001; Hope, 2015; Morganwalp &
Sage, 2011; Rouhani, Nikpay, & Mohamaddoust, 2014),and Enterprise
architecture evaluation process (Nikpay, Ahmad, Kia et al., 2017).
2.1. EA quality attributes
In the context of EA products and services, quality is deﬁned as the
degree to which EA products and services meet EA stakeholder's needs.
These stakeholders include individuals and groups, ranging from top
management and architects to project managers and business users both
inside and outside of an organization (Neimi & Pekkola, 2013). Some
EA research focused on the EA quality attributes to evaluate EA quality.
Simonsson et al. (2005) presented EA Evaluation indicators in a one-
level model, and used measurable criteria. Their model is simply
quantiﬁable and has some indicators, including quality of data, IT-
business alignment, accuracy of data, accessibility of data, eﬃciency,
scalability, system complexity, modiﬁability, and architectural changes.
This method compares some proposed EA solutions for an organization.
Gammelgård et al. (2007) also presented an EA evaluation model. In
their model, functional and non-functional quality attributes are clas-
siﬁed into two categories. Functional attributes are measured based on
the reference models for each industry. Non-functional attributes are
measured based on a completed questionnaire. The qualitative attri-
butes of their model include the following characteristics: availability
and reliability, data quality, information security, interoperability,
modiﬁability, performance and usability. Regarding the goal of en-
terprise architecture, Khayami (2011) tried to give a model for deﬁning
a good enterprise architecture that can be used for evaluation. He
suggested eight quality attributes of EA including alignment, con-
vergence, maintainability, integrity, reliability, eﬃciency, security, and
usability. In the Neimi & Pekkola (2013) research, the quality attributes
of EA products and services were identiﬁed and deﬁned. They identiﬁed
a set of ten quality attributes including clarity and conciseness, gran-
ularity, uniformity and cohesion, availability, correctness, and useful-
ness that are related to EA products, and also availability and timing,
awareness, activeness, and usefulness that are related to EA services,

2.2. Software architecture analysis
An enterprise architecture linked with four layers including busi-
ness, information, application, and infrastructure layers, so EA mea-
surement indicators should be related to each of the four mentioned
layers (Hämäläinen, 2008; Niemi, Ylimäki, & Hämäläinen, 2008).
Software architecture (SA) has been proposed as an important approach
for managing software system (Saat et al., 2011) that has a place in
application layers of EA. In fact, SA emphases on one area of enterprise
architecture describing more detailed structural decisions, especially
concerning technical structure of information systems (Hämäläinen,
2008). SA shows information about all dimensions of EA in more details
from the perspective of information systems. Therefore, it has sig-
niﬁcantly narrower scope than EA. It deﬁnes the components of a
system, their interrelationships, external connections, and crucial
principles of development (Bass, Clements, & Kazman, 1998). As a re-
sult, SA evaluation must be considered in an EA evaluation.
Based on the ISO 9126 standard, the quality attributes of an IT-
system containing performance or eﬃciency, interoperability, avail-
ability, security, usability, accuracy, maintainability, and suitability.
These quality attributes have also been mentioned in the book titled
enterprise architecture –models and analyses for information systems
decision making (Johnson & Ekstedt, 2007). Närman et al. (2007)
identiﬁed a system quality analysis framework in the form of an ex-
tended inﬂuence diagrams. This framework has aﬀorded a roadmap for
analyzing the system quality that can illustrate some EA attributes in
the application layer and also includes the following attributes: se-
curity, eﬃciency, reliability, usability, interoperability, maintain-
ability, suitability, and accuracy.
2.3. EA eﬀectiveness assessment
The EA eﬀectiveness is accomplished when developed EA artefacts
inside the enterprise meet the individual goals of stakeholders, EA
function eﬀectiveness is: "The degree to which organizational objectives
are attained through the outputs of the EA function" (Morganwalp &
Sage, 2011). The most of existing EA assessment approaches focus on
determining the eﬃciency of the EA delivery functions. They emphasize
on the EA processes, the quality of the EA products, the knowledge,
skills, and the architects' experiences (Van der Raadt & Bonnet, 2010).
Buchanan (2001) recommends the EA quality must be measured by
three dimensions including ﬁnancial eﬃciency, business eﬀectiveness,
and architecture process. He provides a list of ﬁnancial eﬃciency and
business eﬀectiveness beneﬁts that should be comprehended when an
organization's EA is of high quality. Morganwalp and Sage (2011)
summarized several author’s perspectives on how to measure the ef-
fectiveness of EA based on the three measurement dimensions and
corresponding beneﬁts of Buchanan's research (Buchanan, 2001). They
formulated 12 qualitative objectives with 58 indicators to be reached by
any EA framework and EA development process. 11 of 12 objectives (47
of 58 indicators) appeared to be positively inﬂuenced by the EA fra-
meworks and the development processes. Bonnet (2009) designed and
veriﬁed an initial conceptual model and an initial EA measurement
model to guide the organizations for assessing the eﬀectiveness of an
EA. According to their model, agility and alignment are two main
measures of EA and each of them has several indicators. The indicators
of agility including external change monitoring, ﬂexibility, quality and
customization, speed, and initiation of change. The indicators of
alignment including internal monitoring, governance, partnership,
conformance and integration, readiness for change, and also commu-
nication and understanding.
Pruijt, Slot, Plessius, Bos, and Brinkkemper (2012) presented a
novel instrument to evaluate how well an EA management function is
able to realize its goals, the Enterprise Architecture Realization Scor-
ecard (EARS). Based on their instrument, during the assessment of an
EA goal, ﬁve types of results are investigated and discussed in inter-
views with relevant stakeholders which delivered during the EA reali-
zation process. The stakeholders' arguments are assembled and trans-
lated to the scores by means of some indicators. For each result, three
aspects are scored: product, acceptance and scope. The scores are re-
corded at a scorecard and subsequently, totals at result level and goal
level can be calculated. Finally, an assessment report with a scorecard,
strengths and weaknesses of the EA realization process is prepared.
2.4. Enterprise architecture evaluation process
Nikpay, Ahmad, Kia et al. (2017) declared there is a dearth of
models to enable EA evaluation eﬀectiveness by considering the entire
EA, and the motivation of the EA evaluation studies rely on certain
types of quality factors, alignment, metrics, and understanding of EA
evaluation. Nikpay, Ahmad, Kia et al. (2017), Nikpay, Ahmad, Rouhani
et al. (2017) developed a hybrid evaluation method for EA im-
plementation in order to evaluate all aspects of EA through three phases
consequently: preparation, development, and evaluation. The ﬁrst
phase provides the foundation of the hybrid method through a sys-
tematic literature review and interviews with EA practitioners and also
some factors that eﬀect on EA implementation evaluation. The second
phase emphases on developing a hybrid method for evaluating EA
implementation. In the last phase, the proposed method is validated
using a case study and the expert reviews. Their suggested method
comprises two criteria for evaluating EA implementation, that are
functionality and eﬀectiveness. This method embraces three categories
of practices counting initiation, controlling and sustainability and sev-
eral questions as indicators that describe each practice. The hybrid
method supports the EA project lifecycle including design, management
(development), and maintenance that provides step-by-step guidelines
for enterprise architecture implementation.
2.5. EA maturity evaluation
Ylimäki (2007) proposed a generic EA evaluation model that is
based on the combination of the potential critical success factors (CSFs)
of EA and the maturity stages. Critical success factors including as-
sessment and evaluation, business driven approach, commitment,
communication and common language, development methodology and
tool support, EA models and artifacts, governance, IT investment and
acquisition strategies, organizational culture, project and program
management, scope and purpose, skilled team, and training and edu-
cation. Based on the combination of these factors, the key questions are
allocated to each CSF and the maturity levels to evaluate the stage of
each CSF.

3.1. Qualitative section (SLR)
Given that, this paper used the exploratory mixed method, so in the
ﬁrst step, preceding research related to EA evaluation were studied. SLR
approach has been chosen as research method in order to conduct this
section. This study used the guidelines that were proposed by
Kitchenham and Charters (2007). According to Kitchenham and

three consecutive stages: planning, execution, and result analysis; In
this section, we focus on the planning stage which includes deﬁning the

3.1.1. Review design
This section describes the foundation of this review by deﬁning SLR
research questions and search keywords.
3.1.1.1. SLR research questions. The EA evaluation is not a new
problem. Over the years, diﬀerent approaches, methods and models
have been proposed for that. This research intends to identify indicators
that evaluate EA quality. The SLR research question that we expected to
answer in this research is as follows:
RQ1. What are the EA quality indicators for evaluating an im-
plemented EA?
In this study, the word 'indicator' refers to a variable that deﬁne and
evaluate the quality of an implemented EA in the organization and the
word ‘factor’ refers to the quality attribute of an implemented EA and
includes some indicators which is mentioned in section 3.2.5.
3.1.1.2. Search process. We focus on searching in scientiﬁc databases
rather than in speciﬁc books or technical reports, as we assumed that
the major research results in books and reports are also usually
described in scientiﬁc papers. The following sources have been
selected to execute the SLR search process:
- Science Direct – Elsevier (http://www.elsevier.com).
- Springer Link (http://www.springerlink.com).
- ACM Digital Library (https://dl.acm.org/).
- IEEE Xplore (http://www.ieee.org/web/publications/xplore/).
- Sage Journals (https://journals.sagepub.com).
- Google Scholar (www.scholar.google.com).
The following search keywords were used to ﬁnd relevant studies in
paper’s title, keywords, and abstract: "Enterprise Architecture
Evaluation" or "Enterprise Architecture Measurement" or "Enterprise
Architecture Measurement Indicators" or "Enterprise Architecture
Attributes" or "Enterprise Architecture Quality Attributes" or "Software
Architecture Quality Attributes" or "Software Architecture
Measurement Indicators" or "Software Architecture Evaluation" or
"Software Architecture Measurement".
Despite the two concepts of software architecture and enterprise
architecture are diﬀerent, one of the four main layers of EA is the
software architecture. Therefore, the EA evaluation involves the SA
evaluation. In addition, there are a lot of research that are related to the
SA evaluation. So, the motivation for searching keywords such as
Software Architecture Quality Attributes, Software Architecture
Measurement Indicators, Software Architecture Evaluation, Software
Architecture Measurement can identify the SA quality attributes that
can be seen as the EA quality attributes.
3.1.2. Review conduction
This section deﬁnes the review protocol for conducting the SLR. The
SLR review protocol refers to the structure and rules of conducting the
review.
3.1.2.1. Inclusion and exclusion criteria. The goal of setting up criteria is
to discover all related studies of this research. We have focused on full
papers in English from peer-reviewed journals, conferences, workshops
and also master and doctoral theses. We did not set a lower boundary
on the year of publication because we intended to include all relevant
studies that were stored in databases over the years. We excepted
studies that do not explicitly relate to EA evaluation, SA evaluation, EA
quality attributes and SA quality attributes. Furthermore, when several
duplicated articles of a study exist in diﬀerent versions that appear as
books, journal papers, conference and workshop papers, the most
complete version of the study was included. A summary of the
inclusion and exclusion criteria are:
Inclusion criteria:
- English peer-reviewed studies that answer to the research questions
- Studies that focus on EA evaluation or EA measurement
- Studies that focus on SA analysis and/or SA quality analysis
- Studies that focus on EA quality attributes and/or SA quality attri-
butes
Exclusion criteria:
- Studies are not in English
- Studies that are not related to the research questions
- Duplicated studies
3.1.2.2. Study selection. Once the relevant primary studies have been
identiﬁed, they need to be assessed for their actual relevance. In this
regard, the selection of studies is performed through the following
processes:
- Search in databases to identify relevant studies using the search
keywords.
- Exclude studies based on the exclusion criteria.
- Exclude irrelevant studies based on analysis of their titles and ab-
stracts.
- Evaluating the selected studies based on full text read.
- Re-evaluating the results in random.
- Obtain primary studies.
3.1.2.3. Quality assessment. To guide the clariﬁcation of the ﬁndings in
the included studies, and control the strength of inferences, we used the
following quality criteria for appraising the selected studies.
- The data analysis of the study is precise and based on evidence or
theoretical reasoning instead of non-justiﬁed statements.
- The study has a description of the context in which the research was
carried out.
- The purposes of the study are supported by the design and execution
of research.
To make certain our conﬁdence in the credibility of a particular
identiﬁed study and its relevance for data synthesis in the review, all
the included studies met each of the three criteria.

3.2. Quantitative section (statistical analysis)
The objective of the quantitative sections was validating the iden-
tiﬁed EA quality attributes and their indicators by EA experts` opinions
through a statistical analysis that led a more precise model for evalu-
ating an implemented EA.
3.2.1. Data collection
In order to validating the identiﬁed EA quality indicators, the ex-
perts 'opinions were collected using a ﬁve-point Likert-type ques-
tionnaire that was ranged from (1) "strongly disagree" to (5) "strongly
agree". Questions of this questionnaire were prepared directly by Table
S5 (Appendix C in Supplementary Material). The questionnaire was
distributed to respondents after reviewing the factual and content va-
lidity by EA experts and EA professors.
The statistical society of this research was the chief enterprise ar-
chitects of organizations that were members of the Enterprise
Architecture and Information Technology Association in Iran. EA pro-
ject was implemented in all of their organizations. There were nearly
300 organizations. So, we asked the chief enterprise architects to re-
spond to the questionnaire. Although, based on Morgan's table, 169
experts had to be considered as sample, 189 questionnaires were sent
and 168 questionnaires were answered (The rate of replication was
0.888). Finally, data was analyzed by exploratory and conﬁrmatory
factor analysis.
3.2.2. Descriptive data analysis
In order to get acquainted with the respondents, their demographic
variables, such as: gender, education, their awareness of enterprise
architecture, the EA implementation experience or the strategic IT
planning experience based on EA were detailed. The results indicated
that 13.7 % of the respondents were of female group and 86.3 % were
male. The education level of 13.7 % of the respondents was doctorate,
63.7 % of master, 22 % of bachelor and 0.6 % associate. The re-
spondents' awareness level of EA was 11.9 % of very high, 40.5 % high,
43.5 % moderate and 4.2 % low. 65 % of respondents had the EA im-
plementation experience or the strategic IT planning experience based
on EA and 34.5 % had no experience.
Before performing factor analysis, the reliability of the measure-
ment tool was obtained using Cronbach's alpha coeﬃcient equal to
0.89, which was acceptable.
3.2.3. Exploratory factor analysis (EFA)
In EFA, the explorer has no expectations of the number or nature of
the variables and as the title suggests, is exploratory in nature. It allows
the researcher to explore the main dimensions of the variables to gen-
erate a theory, or model from a relatively large set of latent constructs
often represented by a set of items. EFA also helps to reduce the large
volume of variables to a limited number of factors, which, due to a
common source of variance, explain the covariance of that variable
(Williams, Brown, & Andrys, 2010).
Questions of the questionnaire must be correlated to a certain ex-
tent, and excessive correlation prevents the extraction of independent
factors. On the other hand, if the correlation between the questions goes

3.2.4. Research hypotheses
Alignment and integrity: "alignment can be deﬁned as managing
and utilizing IT in an organization in order to respond to business
needs, achieve business goals and acquire competitive advantages
(Alaeddini, 2017)". Enterprise integration is the process of certifying
the interaction between enterprise entities (such as enterprise systems)
to achieve domain objectives (Nikpay, Ahmad, Rouhani et al., 2017).
Hypothesis 1. enterprise architecture is eﬀective on alignment and
integrity.
Maintainability and portability: EA maintainability is deﬁned as
the ease which EA components can be modiﬁed to correct faults, im-
prove performance or other attributes, or adapt to a changed environ-
ment (Razavi & Aliee, 2009). EA portability is the capability of the
software product to be transferred from one environment to another.
The environment may include organizational, hardware or software
environment (Losavio, Chirinos, Matteo, Levy, & Ramdane-Cherif,
2004)
Hypothesis 2. enterprise architecture is eﬀective on maintainability
and portability.
Reliability: reliability is the ability of a system to keep operating
over time without failure. Some factors having an impact on system
reliability are the currency of data backup, the level of hardware re-
dundancy, and the quality of the IT organization (Närman et al., 2007).
Hypothesis 3. enterprise Architecture is eﬀective on reliability.
Reusability: EA reusability displays the extent of hardware com-
ponents (infrastructure subassemblies, common client platforms for
well-deﬁned), types of users (servers run as multiple instances of a
single image), software components (system software components,
middleware components, business-speciﬁc components, application
components and variants, architecture technical domains) and methods
(architecture analyses and process model, object-oriented business and
software engineering methods, system and software test methods, en-
gineering tool methods) are usable (Morganwalp & Sage, 2011).
Hypothesis 4. enterprise architecture is eﬀective on reusability.
Scalability: EA scalability is the ability to grow/shrink the capa-
city/performance of a system according to the demand of the en-
vironment where it operates (Simonsson et al., 2005).
Hypothesis 5. enterprise architecture is eﬀective on scalability.
Security: the issue of protection of information and application
systems from unauthorized access, use, disclosure, disruption, mod-
iﬁcation and destruction in order to provide conﬁdentiality, integrity
and availability (Khayami, 2011).
Hypothesis 6. enterprise architecture is eﬀective on security.
The quality of EA products and services: the quality of EA pro-
ducts and services is deﬁned as a set of a product or service char-
acteristics that have an eﬀect on its ability to satisfy customer needs
(Neimi & Pekkola, 2013).
Hypothesis 7. enterprise architecture is eﬀective on the quality of EA
products and services.
3.2.5. Conﬁrmatory factor analysis (CFA)
In statistics, conﬁrmatory factor analysis is a special form of factor
analysis that is used to test whether measures of a construct are
consistent with a researcher's understanding of the nature of that con-
struct (or factor). In the second-order conﬁrmatory factor model, each
of the factors which is determined by the observed variables are in-
ﬂuenced by other latent variables at a higher level. Consequently, in
addition to investigate the relationship between observable variables
and factors, the relationship between latent variables and factors is also
examined (Chin, Marcolin, & Newsted, 1996). In this study all factors
(quality attributes) including alignment and integrity, the quality of EA
products and services, security, maintainability and portability, reusa-
bility and scalability measure EA (as a latent variable). Measurement
and structural models are commonly used for CFA and the researchers
must obey the requirement needed to achieve the true model. In this
section, the ﬁtness of the measurement and structural model are sur-
veyed by using SmartPLS.
3.2.5.1. Evaluation of measurement model. A measurement model must
be assessed with the reliability and validity. The reliability is evaluated
through three ways: evaluation of factors loading, Cronbach's alpha
coeﬃcient and composite reliability. A suitable factor loading values is
greater than 0.4. Unlike, the value of Cronbach`s alpha coeﬃcient
greater than 0.70 indicate that the measurement model is reliable
(Nunnally, 1978). The composite reliability can vary between 0 and 1
and its values larger than 0.6 are frequently judged as acceptable (e.g.,
Bagozzi and Yi, 1988). As results in Table 6, according to the three
mentioned values, we conclude that the reliability value of the model is
acceptable.
For assessing the validity of the used instrument, this study focused
on the convergent and discriminant validity (Hulland, 1999, p. 199).
For the convergent validity, researchers need to examine the average
variance extracted (AVE). According to Fornell and Larcker (1981), an
AVE value of 0.50 and higher indicates a suﬃcient degree of the con-
vergent validity, meaning that the latent variable (constructs) explains
more than half of its indicator’s variances. They also postulate that a
latent construct shares more variance with assigned indicators than
with another latent variable in the structural model.
The discriminant validity test indicates that how variance of the
indicators is able to explain variance in the construct. The discriminant
validity value is obtained from the square root of AVE value. The di-
agonal values are the square root of AVE while other values are the
correlation between the respective constructs (Afthanorhan, 2013). As
results indicate in Table 6, the convergent validity values of the model
are acceptable. The discriminant validity test of research model states
that each of variables interacts more with their questions than with
questions of other variables; in other words, the discriminant validity
value of the model is appropriate.
Kolmogorov–Smirnov test is used for evaluating the normality of the
research data. It was found that the distribution of all research variables
data does not follow the normal statistical distribution except enterprise
architecture variable. Spearman's rank correlation coeﬃcient was used
to measure the correlation between enterprise architecture variable and
the other variables (7 identiﬁed factors), the correlation signiﬁcance
levels between all variables were less than 0.05 that indicate a positive
signiﬁcant correlation between all the variables.

3.2.6. Model validation
A global criterion of goodness of ﬁt (i.e., GoF index) has been pro-
posed by Tenenhaus, Amato, and Esposito (2004). The intent is to ac-
count for the PLS model performance and validation at both the mea-
surement model and the structural model with a focus on overall
prediction performance of the model. The GoF index is obtained as the
geometric mean of the average communality index and the average R2
value. Three values of 0.01, 0.25 and 0.36 were reported as weak,
moderate and strong values for GOF. the GOF criterion was equivalent
to 0.715, which indicates a strong ﬁtness of the overall research model.
3.2.7. EA quality attributes ranking
In order to rank EA quality attributes and its indicators, after
identifying seven EA quality attributes and its indicators, ten EA experts
were asked to determine importance of each indicators by a ques-
tionnaire and collected data were surveyed through TOPSIS1
method.
Base on TOPSIS method the basic principle is that the chosen alter-
native should have the shortest distance from the ideal solution and the
farthest distance from the negative-ideal solution. The best alternative
is one that has a higher value, because it is closer to a positive ideal
solution (Jahanshahloo, Lotﬁ, & Izadikhah, 2006). The closeness coef-
ﬁcient and rank of the EA quality attributes are shown in Table 8, As
results indicate " The quality of EA products and services" is the most
important and " Security” is the least important EA quality attribute. In

5. Discussion and future work
The lack of attention to achieve a holistic model for evaluating an
implemented EA can lead to insuﬃcient and inaccurate analysis of EA
performance quality. EA evaluations research often did not consider the
enterprise architecture itself, but rather measure elements that are re-
lated to EA (Andersen et al., 2014). For example, services (Närman,
Holm, Ekstedt, & Honeth, 2013), applications (Närman, Holm, Höök,
Honeth, & Johnson, 2012), processes, enterprise systems (Lagerström,
Johnson, & Ekstedt, 2010), architectural candidates (Razavi, Aliee, &
Tafreshi, 2009; Razavi, Aliee, & Badie, 2010), beneﬁts (Plessius, Slot, &
Pruijt, 2012) or projects (Quartel, Steen, & Lankhorst, 2012). Moreover,
enterprise architecture evaluation methods, metrics and models are
more established on the level of SA rather than EA (Niemi et al., 2008).

limitation and there is a deﬁnite need for more accurate and objective
measures for diﬀerent aspects of an implemented EA. For example,
Närman et al. (2007) presented a meta model for analyzing enterprise
system quality that is multi-level and deﬁnite in SA domain, some of
quality attributes (EA product & service quality, scalability, and port-
ability) are not considered in their model. The proposed model of the
present research has completely investigated quality attributes.
Gammelgård et al. (2007) also proposed a one-level quality model in
the form of a questionnaire that its indicators are very qualitative and
make evaluation diﬃcult. Furthermore, their model suggests to use the
business model of each industry for aligning their IT and business, so if
there is a business model for an industry, this model can be a powerful
tool, but in the absence of a business model, their model does not
evaluate EA suitably. In addition, their model is more deﬁned in SA
domain. Lim, Lee, and Park (2009) determined more EA quality attri-
butes by focusing on all aspects of EA and presented a comparative
analysis of EA frameworks based on EA quality attributes. It can be
helpful to select a suitable EA Framework, which meets the quality
requirements but it is diﬃcult to consider their deﬁned attributes as EA
indicators for evaluating an implemented EA, because the identiﬁed
indicators are very qualitative. Simonsson et al. (2005) presented EA
indicators in a one-level model did not consider some attributes (EA
product & service quality, reliability, integrity). In Addition, the in-
dicators are small in number. The proposed model of this paper covers
Simonson et al. model and propounds more aspects of EA to evaluate.
Neimi & Pekkola (2013) cleared 10 quality attributes for EA products
and services and sub-factors for further validating of EA in diverse or-
ganizations with diﬀerent EA products and EA services. Their work
presents a large extent of the EA quality criteria. But problematically, it
only presents the nonfunctional features of EA products, services and
processes that comprises the overall quality of EA, neglecting the aspect
of implemented EA.

5.1. Future work
This study contributes to EA research by providing a set of EA
quality attribute to evaluate and improve it in organizations. In order to
continue this research, the presented evaluation indicators can be
combined with EA frameworks or be expanded and developed.
Allocating weight and signiﬁcance for indicators and each of the eva-
luation quality attribute using multi-criteria analysis or other methods
can be included in the future research agenda. In addition, by ranking
each of the quality attribute and indicators and identifying some levels
or by deﬁning a range for each of them in organization, a maturity
model for the EA quality can be developed which can be considered as a
separate research. This research can be done using more data from
various industries. Also, measuring EA for a particular industry based
on the model of this paper, can be considered as a research topic in the
future, for the reason that some organizations in diﬀerent industries
implement a speciﬁc enterprise architecture framework related to their
industry and follow their industry rules and policies.
6. Conclusion
Measurement and evaluation capabilities and skills are seen as es-
sential for enterprise architecture and its adaptation. Development of
these capabilities and skills is a current concern of many architecture
group and architects in companies. Evaluation of EA generates in-
formation, which supports planning, improving, marketing (showing
value), Organizing and enterprise architecture managing in an organi-
zation. It generates information to support decision making on en-
terprise- wide information system issues through the analysis of the
enterprise architecture models too. By exploring the existing evaluation
models, it was realized that there is no comprehensive, structured and
validated model for evaluating EA status or success of EA im-
plementation, which consider all layers of EA. In this paper we in-
vestigated the question of What are the EA quality indicators for eval-
uating an implemented EA. Through a careful review of EA evaluation
literature and related academic theory and also performing a statistical
analysis, this study has proposed a model for evaluating EA, which can
be used as a checklist in the form of a questionnaire including EA
