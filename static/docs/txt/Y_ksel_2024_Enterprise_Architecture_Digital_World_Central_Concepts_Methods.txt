Preface
The economy is changing at a rapid pace due to digitization, and there is no end in sight.
The topic of digital transformation has become a fixed component of internal company
projects and changes. This constantly presents companies, executives, and employees
with major challenges. Many CIOs and IT executives have to adapt to new digital eco-
systems, business models, and ways of working. Existing things must be reviewed and
regularly adapted to innovations from the digital world. This is where enterprise archi-
tects come into play. They operate at the interface between business customers, i.e., the
departments within their own company, and IT. They have an eye on the big picture and
at the same time, they set the course for strategic (architecture) decisions. Somewhat
exaggerated, one could say: The success of a company in the digital world depends on
the quality of the decisions of its enterprise architects and the speed of implementation.
In an unstable, uncertain, complex, and ambiguous “VUCA” world, as an enterprise
architect, it is necessary to consider everything and maintain an overview. The perspec-
tives of other people must be purposefully included in order to understand the big pic-
ture, uncover blind spots, and make better decisions. This has resulted in far-reaching
challenges for enterprise architects in the design of their perspective, work, and mode of
operation over the past few years.
In light of these challenges, I want to introduce specific and partly new perspectives
for the EAM method in this book. They are intended to help understand the implications
of digital change and to show EAs possible approaches to continue to be successful in
the age of digitization. The statements in this book are based on my own experiences
from many digitization and EAM projects and on insights from an intensive exchange
with a large number of experts from both companies and corresponding consulting firms
as well as from academia. In this book, central terms and concepts from epistemology,
semiotics, and neuropsychology are introduced into enterprise architecture management.
A model from semiotics is presented and used to analyze and describe complex software
and enterprise systems with its help. From neuropsychology, basic functions of our brain
when making decisions are presented and used to make strategic architecture decisions
better. In addition, a positive image of humans and their fundamental abilities is brought
into enterprise architecture management from neuropsychology. Among other things,

About the Author
Dr. Hüseyin Yüksel, Born in 1974, he is married and the
father of two children. He studied in Darmstadt and earned
his doctorate in Plymouth, England. For over 20 years, he
has been active as an entrepreneur and consultant, while also
dedicating himself intensively to teaching and research. To
this day, he advises on transformation projects in numerous
large corporations as an enterprise architect and supports
executives in the introduction of new working methods. His
basic attitude is: “Empower people!” Together with his part-
ners from “likeminds”, he helps large corporations to bring
more humanity into the working world with new working
methods. As a business coach, he helps start-ups in Hub 31
in Darmstadt to build a sustainable company. In parallel, he
is a lecturer in the Department of Computer Science and
Business at the TU Darmstadt and the Darmstadt University
ofApplied Sciences.
