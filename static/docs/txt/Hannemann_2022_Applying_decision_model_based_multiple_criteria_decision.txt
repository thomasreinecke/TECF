Abstract
Organisations all over the world are going through the process of digital transformation
(DT). Enterprise Architecture (EA) is a method and an organising principle that aligns
the business's objectives and strategies with the Information Technology strategy and
execution plan. EA provides a guide to direct the evolution and transformation of en-
terprises with technology. The EA principles are one of the key concepts in the definition
of EA; they assist in recognizing the organization vision and validating the outcomes.
However, the lack of adequate instruments for assessing the current state and identifying
opportunities for EA management procedures improvement often leave organisations
unsure of where to begin improving their procedures. The aim of this paper is to help
organisations identify these improvement opportunities. To do so, a decision model was
developed to evaluate the influence DT technologies have on the EA principles proposed
by The Open Group Architecture Framework (TOGAF). A literature review was con-
ducted, and five main DT Technologies applied in the EA scope were identified. With
that, a decisional model was created based on two decision‐making methods called
Decision‐Making Trial and Evaluation Laboratory and PROMETHEE. The 21 archi-
tecture principles proposed by TOGAF were evaluated and the influence the technolo-
gies exercised on the principles were identified. As a result, Big Data and Cloud
Computing technologies were indicated as having the greatest effect over the analysed
principles, therefore concluding that when applied in the EA scope, these technologies
can help organisations improve their EA procedures.

1 | INTRODUCTION
Organisations all over the world are going through the process
of digital transformation (DT). According to Nightingale e
Rhodes [1], enterprises are complex, highly integrated systems
comprised of processes, organisations, information and sup-
porting technologies, with multifaceted interdependencies and
interrelationships across their boundaries. Understanding, en-
gineering, and managing these complex social, technical, and
infrastructure dimensions are critical in achieving and sus-
taining enterprise performance [1]. To provide such support
and attack the Business and Information Technology (IT)
alignment problem, Enterprise Architecture (EA) has emerged.
Taking a holistic approach, EA focusses not only on the
technical aspects but also on the various aspects of the en-
terprise upon which the IT systems operate [2].
EA is a method and an organising principle that combines
functional business objectives and strategies with an ITstrategy

2 | LITERATURE REVIEW
According to Xiao and Watson [8], a literature review is an
essential feature of academic research. The knowledge
advancement must be based on existing work. In reviewing the
relevant literature, we understand the breadth and depth of the
existing studies and identify gaps to be explored.
A literature review was conducted to discover the main DT
technologies applied to the scope of EA. The first step was to
identify the keywords that would be used in the research. Axis
1 contemplates words referring to the evaluation universe of
the problem space while, Axis 2, includes words relevant to the
second evaluation space. The keywords used in the research are
presented in Table 1.
Once the keywords were identified, the following research
criteria were defined: (i) Papers limited to the last 5 years, from
2016 to 2020; (ii) selection of papers available in the English
language and (iii) exclusion of papers that do not include the
author's name. A search using the defined keywords was per-
formed in the SCOPUS database, generating a result of 1531
articles. After applying the research criteria mentioned above,
505 articles remained. Then a series of analyses were carried
out and as a result, 32 articles remained. Table 2 includes the
research criteria applied in the selection of papers.
The 32 remaining articles were introduced in the NVivo
software [9] and the word‐count tool was applied to determine
the most mentioned technologies. As a result, the software
generated a cluster analysis (Figure 1) of the most mentioned
words.
By analysing the results generated, five main technologies
in the scope of I4.0 and EA were identified: Cloud Computing,
Internet of Things (IoT), Artificial Intelligence (AI), Big Data
and Cyber‐Physical systems (CPS). Table 3 includes the tech-
nologies and their descriptions.
3 | MATERIALS AND METHODS
3.1 | Enterprise architecture (EA)
An EA presents the structure of an enterprise and consists of
the main enterprise components such as a company's goals,
organizational structures, information infrastructure and busi-
ness process. It helps the organization determine how it can
effectively achieve its current and future goals. The perfor-
mance of an enterprise, such as innovations generated within
the company, the re‐engineering of business processes, and the
quality and timeliness of information flow, can be improved if
the EA system faithfully represents the characteristics and the
nature of the organization [15]. According to Korhonen e
Halén [16], EA has been suggested as a discipline to ensure a
coherent structure, to make visible the underlying organiza-
tional system, and to facilitate change.

Currently, there are many EA frameworks that can be
used by organisations or companies according to their
needs. The four most widely used frameworks in the in-
dustry today are the Zachman Framework, Gartner
Framework, Federal Enterprise Architecture Framework, and
TOGAF [17].
TOGAF is a comprehensive open EA standard that con-
tains several components, amongst which, the Architecture
Development Method (ADM) stands at its core. The ADM
describes an iterative process for developing EA with the help
of several phases (Preliminary phase, Architecture Vision,
Business, Information systems, Technology, Opportunities and
solutions, Migration planning, Implementation governance,
Architecture change management, and Requirements
management).
Four architecture domains are covered by TOGAF: (a)
Business architecture, (b) Data architecture, (c) Application
architecture and (d) Technology architecture. These are
commonly accepted as subsets of an overall EA [18]. The
TOGAF framework, a standard of The Open Group, is a
proven EA methodology and framework used by the world's
leading organisations to improve business efficiency [19].
The Zachman Framework for Enterprise Architecture
published by John Zachman in 1987 is considered as one of
the pioneers in this domain [20]. The Framework is based
around the principles of classical architecture that establish a
common vocabulary and set of perspectives for describing
complex enterprise systems [21]. The Zachman Framework has
six perspectives or views: Planner, Owner, Designer, Builder,
Subcontractor, and User. The second dimension of the

3.2 | Enterprise architecture principles
According to The Open Group (2011), ‘Architecture Principles
define the underlying general rules and guidelines for the use
and deployment of all IT resources and assets across the en-
terprise. They reflect a level of consensus among the various
elements of the enterprise and form the basis for making
future IT decisions’.
According to Kempegowda e Chaczko [5], principles assist
in obtaining the organization's vision and validating the out-
comes. It gives the team the ability to decide while executing
the EA framework and provides governance to verify and audit
EA implementation. With the significant increase of technol-
ogies such as Cloud, Big Data, Social Media, Internet of
Things, Robotic Process Automation, Analytics and so forth, it
is critical to define Principles that are easy to understand, broad
enough and practical to use [7]. Architecture principles are
largely produced, accepted and valued, but not largely
approved and effectively used by enterprise architects, de-
signers, developers, CIOs and managers [25].
The Open Group Architecture Framework (TOGAF),
being an open standard free to use framework has showed
wide acceptance in the organisations, for that matter, in this
present work the 21 Principles defined by TOGAF were used
to develop the decision model to evaluate the influence DT
technologies have on the architecture principles proposed by
TOGAF. The current version 9.2 released in 2018 contain the
same principles in the version 9.0 released in 2009.
3.3 | Digital transformation
Digital Transformation is defined as ‘the use of technology to
radically improve performance or reach of enterprises’ [26].
Another well‐known definition of the term is that it ‘is ach-
ieved when the digital usages, which have been developed
enable innovation and creativity and stimulate significant
changes within the professional or knowledge domain’ [27].
The term Industry 4.0 (I4.0), also known as smart
manufacturing, was first mentioned at the Hannover fair in
2011. Lu [28] summarises industry 4.0 as an integrated,
adapted, optimised, service‐oriented, and interoperable
manufacturing process, which is correlated with algorithms, big
data, and high technologies. It facilitates inter‐connection and
computerisation into the traditional industry. According to
Liao et al. [29], the modelling and virtualisation/visualization
technologies have been the two most frequent enabling tech-
nologies applied to support the realization of Industry 4.0. The
goals of Industry 4.0 are to provide IT‐enabled customisation
of manufactured products; to make automatic and flexible
adaptation of the production chain; to track parts and prod-
ucts; to facilitate communication among parts, products, and
machines; to apply human‐machine interaction paradigms; to
achieve IoT‐enabled production optimisation in smart fac-
tories; and to provide new types of services and business
models of interaction in the value chain.

3.4 | Multi‐criteria decision‐making
methods
According to the International Society on Multiple Criteria
Decision Making (MCDM), ‘MCDM is the study of methods
and procedures by which concerns about multiple conflicting
criteria can be formally incorporated into the management
planning process’ [7]. According to Cestari et al. [30], an
MCDM helps the decision makers in understanding his pref-
erences (using criteria) to choose an adequate alternative or
prioritise and ranking a set or alternatives. When structuring
the decision‐making process, it is essential to understand the
type of problems in which the decision is inserted. The type of
problem will guide the choice of the multi‐criteria method to
be used. With that said, two methods were appointed as the
most suitable for this research context.
One of the methods chosen for this research is the
Decision‐Making Trial and Evaluation Laboratory (DEMA-
TEL) methodology proposed by Gabus and Fontela in 1971.
This method has been applied to demonstrate the in-
terrelations among criteria and to find aspects/criteria that
play central roles in the system to represent the effectiveness
of them [31]. In general, the DEMATEL technique can
determine direct, indirect, and interdependence among the
unpredictable features or attributes [32]. The DEMATEL
method was chosen because it identifies which attributes
have the greatest influence on others as well as the attributes
that are most influenced. The method is composed of six
steps [33]:
I) Gather experts' opinion and calculate the average
matrix Z;
II) Calculatethenormalisedinitialdirect‐relationmatrixD;
III) Derive the total relation matrix T;
IV) Calculate the sums of rows and columns of matrix T;
V) Set a threshold value ðαÞ;
VI) Build a cause‐and‐effect relationship diagram.
The PROMETHEE (Preference Ranking Organization
Method for Enrichment Evaluation) method was also selected
for this research. It is a multiple‐criteria decision making
method developed by Brans et al. [34 35]. This method was
chosen because it is a straightforward ranking method in
conception and application compared with other methods
used for multiple‐criteria analysis. It is well adapted to prob-
lems where a finite number of alternatives are to be ranked
according to several, sometimes conflicting criteria [36]. The
evaluation table is the starting point of the PROMETHEE
method. In this table, the alternatives are evaluated on the
different criteria. The implementation of PROMETHEE
requires two additional types of information, namely: (1) In-
formation on the relative importance that is the weights of the
criteria considered. (2) Information on the decision‐maker's
preference function, which he/she uses when comparing the
contribution of the alternatives in terms of each separate
criterion.
3.5 | Decision model structuring process
To develop a decision model to evaluate the influence DT
technologies have on the architecture principles proposed by
TOGAF, a process composed of three phases was structured
(Figure 2). In the first phase, the main technologies in the
context of EA were identified. In the second phase the
DEMATEL method was used to carry out an influence ana-

method was applied to structure and evaluate the Decision
Model. Each of the phases will be further detailed in the next
section.
3.5.1 | Definition of the main technologies in the
context of enterprise architecture
To identify the main technologies in the scope of EA, a liter-
ature review was conducted and detailed in section 2 of this
paper. Five main technologies in the scope of I4.0 and EA were
identified: Cloud Computing, Internet of Things (IoT), AI, Big
Data and CPS.

influence analyses
The influence analysis of the EA Principles is grounded on the

During the execution stage of this research the experts' opinion
were gathered to calculate the average matrix Z, with that in-

addition to the attributes already mentioned, it was necessary
to choose how the alternatives would be evaluated for each
criterion. In the ‘Min/Max’ field, the Maximum was chosen,
that is, the higher the performance of the alternative in that
criterion, the greater the influence of that technology for it.
The scale used in the evaluation was a qualitative 5‐point scale,
varying in the following levels of influence: 0 (no influence), 1
(low influence), 2 (medium influence), 3 (high influence) and 4
(influence very high), respectively. The model structured in the
software is shown in Figure 4.
For each alternative, two indexes were calculated: the
positive flow (Φ+), which represents how much a certain
alternative is better than the others and the negative flow (Φ−),

others [38]. With the data application, the ‘Phi’ values were
generated for each of the technologies through the software,
that is, the positive and negative flow indexes and the total flow

In this extended version of the paper [39] the preceding
research was enhanced. The DEMATEL method was
approached to enrich the assessment. The method compares
the influence between each architecture principles and iden-
tifies which principles have the greatest influence on others.
The values obtained from the method served as weights for the
PROMETHEE method; that way, the architecture principles
that cause the most influence on other principles would have
greater weights inputted on the PROMETHEE structure,
causing a greater impact on the results.
In this paper, both results obtained from the first and
second part of the research were compared. When the
DEMATEL weights were not contemplated, the gap between
the flow values obtained by the two most influential technol-
ogies was bigger. In this second part of the research where the
DEMATEL weights are considered, Big Data and Cloud
Computing have a smaller gap between the flows. Even though
the final ranking did not change, it is possible to conclude that
when the DEMATEL weights are considered, both technol-
ogies cause similar influence on the analysed principles.
These technologies have been highly addressed in the ac-
ademic field and applied in organisations. According to Malik,
Rashid and Wani [40], Cloud Computing eliminates the need to
have a complete software and hardware infrastructure to meet
customers' requirements and applications. With this, principles
such as Common Use Applications; Technological Indepen-
dence; Ease‐of‐Use; Technical Diversity Control, are directly
impacted by using this technology.
According to a study conducted by Chen and Zhang [41],
the authors state that 51% of the companies assessed believe
that Big Data can impact the increase in operational efficiency
and that 36% of the associations assume that this technology
can help non‐strategic direction of companies. Therefore, two
main principles proposed by TOGAF are impacted: Maximise
Benefits to the Enterprise; and Responsive Change Manage-
ment. Big Data also deals with the principles that involve data
management, such as Information Management is Everyone's
Business; Data is an Asset; Data is Shared; Data is Accessible;
Common Vocabulary and Data Definitions; and Data Security.
6 | CONCLUSION
To help organisations identify opportunities to improve their
EA management procedures, this research proposes a decision
model based on MCDM Methods to assess the impacts of DT
technologies on the architecture principles proposed by
TOGAF. To do so, initially a literature review was carried out
to identify the main DT technologies used in the scope of EA.

The decision model was developed based on two multi‐
criteria decision‐making methods: DEMATEL and PROM-
ETHEE. Both methods help decision makers make more
assertive choices, reducing risks and negative impacts that can
affect the whole organization. The 21 architecture principles
presented by TOGAF were defined as the criteria to be used in
the assessment. In the previous research [39], the DEMATEL
method was not proposed in the decision model; however, in
this extended paper, the results obtained by the DEMATEL
method were taken into consideration in the model. The re-
sults were inputted as weights in the PROMETHEE structure.
The model was applied and, as a result, Big Data and Cloud
Computing technologies were pointed out as those that effect
the most the analysed principles. Although the overall ranking
did not change, there was a reduction in the gap between the
flow values of the two most influential technologies.
To improve the study carried out, it is proposed as a future
research work, to detail the identified technologies. This will
lead to a more assertive assessment and will assist in directing
opportunities for the evolution and transformation of orga-
nisations with technology.
