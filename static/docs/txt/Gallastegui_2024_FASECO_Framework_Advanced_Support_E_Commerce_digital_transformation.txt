A B S T R A C T
Objectives: The primary aim of this study is to develop a framework that identifies the factors, organizational
processes, and mechanisms that facilitate the successful integration of e-commerce within the digital trans­
formation journey of Small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs). The FASECO framework seeks to enhance
strategic alignment, IT infrastructure, customer experience, and data analytics in SMEs, and is compared with
existing theoretical frameworks to provide a holistic solution.
Methods/Approach: The research employs a mixed-methods approach, starting with an extensive literature review
on digital transformation and e-commerce integration in SMEs, followed by qualitative semi-structured in­
terviews with 13 industry professionals. The data is analysed using thematic analysis, significantly enhanced by
Natural Language Processing (NLP) tools, which allowed for the extraction of deeper insights into e-commerce
adoption challenges and organizational dynamics that traditional methods might overlook.
Results: The study reveals that effective e-commerce integration in SMEs requires robust IT infrastructure,
strategic alignment, personalized customer experiences, and data-driven decision-making. The FASECO frame­
work supports SMEs by providing a comprehensive structure that bridges the gap between strategy and tech­
nology, enhancing their digital maturity and competitiveness. The framework’s holistic approach and detailed
comparison with existing models underscore its effectiveness.
Conclusions: The FASECO framework offers a novel approach tailored to SMEs for digital transformation and e-
commerce integration, highlighting the critical role of strategic and technological alignment in achieving digital
maturity and competitive advantage. The use of NLP techniques in this study enhanced the analysis of qualitative
data, providing deeper insights into organizational processes. Future research should explore the adaptation of
this model for micro-SMEs and the integration of emerging technologies.
Introduction
In the context of the Fourth Industrial Revolution, characterized by
the fusion of advanced technologies that blur the boundaries between
the physical, digital, and biological spheres, SMEs face unprecedented
challenges and opportunities. The rapid evolution of digital technologies
has fundamentally transformed the marketplace, requiring SMEs to
adapt to remain competitive and take advantage of new digital oppor­
tunities. SMEs are distinct from larger corporations due to their limited
resources, smaller workforce, and often decentralized decision-making
processes. These characteristics mean that SMEs face unique chal­
lenges in adopting digital technologies, as they must be more agile and
strategic in their approach to digital transformation (Jafari-Sadeghi,
2020). Additionally, the need to align technological adoption with
business processes is more critical for SMEs, as they typically lack the
extensive budgets and infrastructure available to larger organizations
(Pelletier and Cloutier, 2019). The FASECO framework recognizes these
unique characteristics and offers a tailored approach that facilitates the
adoption of e-commerce by addressing both organizational and tech­
nological challenges.
Despite the clear benefits of digital integration, there remains a
significant gap in the digital maturity of SMEs across Europe, especially
in their e-commerce capabilities. For example, while 95 % of large en­
terprises in the EU have adopted digital tools, only 60 % of SMEs have

This research addresses the scientific problem of understanding how
SMEs can effectively integrate digital technologies to enhance their e-
commerce practices and, by extension, their overall digital competi­
tiveness. Specifically, the study aims to investigate the factors and
organizational processes that facilitate e-commerce adoption among
SMEs, particularly considering identified gaps in digital readiness. By
focusing on these gaps, the research seeks to provide a deeper under­
standing of how SMEs can effectively navigate their digital trans­
formation journey. The findings will contribute to a framework that
supports SMEs in aligning their technological capabilities with market
demands, thus addressing the crucial barriers to digital readiness that
often hinder their competitive edge.
In the context of an increasingly digital economy and e-commerce
due to relevance for the promotion of SMEs’ competitiveness and sur­
vival, European and national policies continue to legitimize and
emphasize the potential advantages of the initiatives and decisions that
involve the introduction and implementation of digital technologies. As
the digital economy continues to evolve, the urgency for SMEs to adapt
becomes increasingly critical for their survival and the broader eco­
nomic vitality of the European market. Given that SMEs constitute 99 %
of all businesses in the EU and employ around 100 million people
(Eurostat, 2024), their successful digital transformation is essential for
economic growth and resilience.
In summary, the focus of the proposed study is to address relevant
recurrent shortcomings in studies related to SMEs’ digital trans­
formation, which emerge in the existing literature, with serious impli­
cations for the success of SMEs’ digital business initiatives.
Consequently, this proposal sets out the following general goal: "deter­
mining the factors, organizational processes, and/or mechanisms that
ease or condition SMEs’ e-commerce integration, favouring their
competitiveness through an adequate digital transformation and adop­
tion of new technologies."
This introduction sets the stage for exploring the development and
application of a comprehensive digital framework aimed at empowering
SMEs to improve their e-commerce operations and digital presence,
thereby contributing to closing the digital divide within the European
context.
Literature review
The world economy is at the center of a new revolution whose origin
lies in deploying a set of information and communication technologies
that allow the hybridization of the physical and digital worlds and the
elimination of borders between them. This context has led to talk of a
fourth industrial revolution (Bessant and Tidd, 2023). The digital rev­
olution drives the convergence of computing, artificial intelligence,
universal connectivity, and data. Technological evolution has acceler­
ated massively, driven by an expansion in our ability to store, process,
and communicate information using electronic devices, and highlights
the rapid pace of digitalization and its implications for SMEs (Murray,
2023). From work to leisure, digital technology is changing our habits,
affecting business practices, and how we cooperate and interact with
others (Dwivedi et al.2022). Digital communications, social networks,
e-commerce, and digital companies are constantly evolving, generating
exponential data growth and new value through data management and
use (European Commission, 2020a).
Today’s digital age goes beyond technology use. It involves indus­
trial modernization, advances in data management, rapid changes,
disruption, and social and cultural impacts on production models
(Brynjolfsson, E., and McAfee, A.,2023). Digital transformation (DT) has
produced new dynamics, changing many organizational functions and
creating new processes, customer connections, collaborative environ­
ments, competitors, and value-generation models. It is also a phenom­
enon impacting people and society directly (Hanelt et al.2021; Kraus
et al.2021; Saarikko et al., 2020; Nadkarni and Prügl, 2021). This ca­
pacity for change and the disruptive effects of technology have made
digital transformation a top priority for business leaders. In the
IESE-PENTEO digital transformation study (Zamora et al., 2020), 82 %
of managers believe digital transformation significantly impacts their
organizations.
DT has emerged as a key driver for sustainability in small and
medium-sized enterprises (SMEs). Shehadeh (2024) argues that adopt­
ing digital technologies not only improves operational efficiency but
also enables SMEs to adapt to the demands of the contemporary market.
This capacity for transformation is essential for SMEs to not only survive
but thrive in a competitive business environment that increasingly re­
quires more sustainable practices. Therefore, the integration of digital
technologies becomes a strategic imperative that can guide SMEs toward
a more sustainable future.
The impact of DT on firm performance is a critical aspect that war­
rants attention. In their study, Shehadeh et al. (2024) highlight how
digitalization can significantly influence organizational performance,
although certain factors, such as gender, are often underestimated. This
approach counters the common perception that digital transformation
uniformly affects all businesses. Understanding these nuances is essen­
tial for SMEs to implement transformation strategies that genuinely
reflect their specific needs and contexts, thereby ensuring optimal
performance.
DT is a global trend and an increasingly important survival strategy
for companies (Pereira et al., 2022; Ransbotham, S. et al., 2023).
However, small and medium business entities (SMEs) continue to face
significant challenges, particularly concerning e-commerce, as their
digital capabilities are often lagging. Sometimes such enhancements
require external support from experienced systems integrators and
consultants to achieve e-commerce maturity. Many SMEs remain un­
aware of the benefits or are unable to afford these resources. Thus, due
to increasing competition and the need for competitiveness, the ability
to enhance specific factors becomes an important pathway for SMEs to
facilitate DT (Vrontis et al., 2022; Klein and Todesco, 2021).
DT scope also includes implementing new processes and automation,
technologies for understanding customers, digital collaboration, new
business models, and improved business agility (Li et al., 2021). This
reflects that digital transformation influences technology solutions,
business models, and organizational models. It occurs in a regulatory,
geographic, and competitive environment, showing the complexity of
digital transformation for SMEs depending on their organizational
model, business strategy, and technology adoption. And challenges
result from the lack of capabilities within the SME targeting group
(Shaikh et al., 2021).
The research by Shehadeh et al. (2023) suggests that DT is funda­
mental for firms in the service sector and presents a
moderated-mediation model illustrating how digital strategies can in­
fluence competitive advantage. This model is particularly relevant for
SMEs operating in highly competitive sectors, where differentiation is
crucial. The ability to align digital strategies with business objectives
may be decisive for SMEs to maintain their relevance in the market and
to capitalize on emerging opportunities through innovation.
An empirical analysis conducted by Shehadeh et al. (2024) in the
context of Jordanian Islamic banks demonstrates that DT only enhances
operational efficiency but also optimizes customer experience. This
finding highlights the importance of digitalization as a tool for organi­
zations, including SMEs, to achieve competitive advantages in an
increasingly saturated market. Thus, the implementation of digital
technologies should focus not only on internal efficiency but also on how
these technologies can transform customer relationships, creating sig­
nificant added value.
Alliances play a critical role in helping organizations leverage
external resources, particularly for SMEs undergoing DT. Collaborative
efforts can significantly enhance the capacity of SMEs to implement
technological solutions and navigate the complexities of e-commerce

Open innovation dynamics and SMEs
The concept of open innovation has gained increasing attention in
recent years as a valuable framework for understanding how organiza­
tions, particularly SMEs, can leverage external knowledge and resources
to drive their innovation processes. SMEs often operate under conditions
of bounded rationality (Chesbrough, 2020), where limited resources and
information availability constrain decision-making. This makes them
ideal candidates for adopting open innovation strategies, which allow
them to access external expertise and technological capabilities
(Balsmeier and Woerter, 2019).
Open social innovation, where collaborative efforts between firms
and other societal stakeholders result in new solutions (Leal Filho et al.,
2022), can also play a critical role in helping SMEs navigate the com­
plexities of e-commerce adoption. By engaging with external partners,
SMEs can co-create solutions that address not only business needs but
also broader societal challenges, such as sustainability and inclusivity in
digital platforms.
Additionally, the dynamics of open innovation in delivery platforms,
mobile payments, and other digital ecosystems (West and Bogers, 2017)
have been shown to enhance business model innovation for SMEs. These
platforms reduce transaction costs and increase accessibility to new
markets, thus facilitating the digital transformation process. The
FASECO framework can benefit from incorporating elements of open
innovation engineering (Chesbrough, 2021), where technological solu­
tions are developed through open collaboration between SMEs and
external partners, creating a more agile and adaptable e-commerce
infrastructure.
Digital Economy and Society Index (DESI)
The DESI monitors Europe’s digital performance and tracks EU
countries’ digital competitiveness progress. By providing data on each
Member State’s digitization, it helps identify areas requiring priority
investment and action. Each DESI report combines quantitative data on
five dimensions: connectivity, human capital, internet use, integration
of digital technology (IDT) and digital public services (Russo2020).
It is the IDT indicator that precisely addresses business digitalization
and e-commerce. This indicator also gives importance to the SME sector
by focusing specifically on % SMEs selling online, % SMEs turnover, and
% SMEs selling online cross-border. Online platforms are very important
because they may promote economic growth by enabling sellers to ac­
cess new markets and attract new customers at a lower cost. Also, cross-
border e-commerce benefits from economies of scale, improving
competitiveness, and reducing costs. Spain’s ranking of 17th in this
category, slightly behind the European average, highlights critical areas
for improvement. Currently, 19 % of Spanish SMEs sell online, which is
above the EU average of 18 %. However, only 7 % of these SMEs engage
in cross-border sales, and 9 % of their turnover is derived from online
sales, indicating significant room for growth in these areas (Bánhidi
et al., 2020).
With a DESI score of 60.8 in 2022, Spain ranks 7th among the 27 EU
Member States, above the EU average of 52.3. Spain performed well in
connectivity (75.0) and digital public services (85.0), but challenges
remain in enhancing digital skills and increasing digital technology
integration among SMEs. (Fig. 1). Spain ranks 17th in digital technology
integration, with 19 % of SMEs selling online, slightly above the EU
average of 18 %. However, only 7 % of SMEs engage in cross-border
sales, and 9 % of their turnover is derived from online sales. However,
Spain is also upon the EU average in human capital indicators (58.0),
and in the integration of digital technology with a score of 45.0 (Fig. 2).
Although the overall score is improving, challenges remain, particularly
in enhancing the digital skills of the population and increasing the
digital technology integration among SMEs.
Economically, digital transformation is supported by European
budget proposals, such as cohesion policies’ structured funds (European
Commission, 2022), Horizon Europe’s R&D&I procedures (European
Commission, 2021), and the "Digital Europe" program (DESI, 2022). The
"Next Generation EU" fund provides member states with the necessary
funds for economic resilience post-COVID (European Commission,
2020b). The recovery plan includes digital transformation funds, un­
derstanding its momentum is key to recovery.

Challenges in implementing digital transformation for SMEs
DT of businesses is rapidly discovering new assets and techniques
and managing business activities and processes. For small and medium-
sized enterprises (SMEs), digital transformation opens doors to new
business and employment opportunities and allows them to enhance
their adaptability, production, and manufacturing methods, while
modifying and eventually substituting the use and concentration of re­
sources (Jafari-Sadeghi, 2020).
However, SMEs often face operational difficulties when trying to
take advantage. SMEs often have difficulty identifying and recruiting
professionals, investing in a digital transition plan, or understanding the
right business resolution for developing innovative digital compe­
tencies. SMEs thus possess unique characteristics that complicate the
implementation of digital transformation.
Given the unique characteristics of SMEs and the examples of the
achievements of some of the world’s most famous and innovative digital
giants, it may appear counterintuitive that SMEs might experience issues
in this area. There are numerous reasons for the difficulty in managing
the operational digital transformational process of SMEs. In this as­
sembly, we will concentrate on those unique characteristics that we
believe will significantly affect the digital transformation process and
the ranging issues that tend to prevent most SMEs from realizing the full
potential of their digital business, concentrating especially on external
and internal factors.
There is a wide variety of obstacles to digital transformation. Po­
tential barriers include resources and financial endowments (Pelletier
and Cloutier, 2019); insufficient infrastructure and technology depen­
dence (Shahadat, M.M. et al., 2023); lack of standards and regulations
(Calvino, F. and Criscuolo, C., 2019); lack of knowledge and expertise
(Castagna, F., et al., 2020); and the design of technology solutions
themselves (Indrawati, H.,2020). SMEs are often faced with all these
challenges. Their small size, limited budget, rigorous regulations, and
non-availability of specific technologies tailored to SMEs are part in­
hibitions for a digital transformation process. The shortage of skills and
expertise at SMEs is exacerbated by the predominantly manual processes
carried out. Small businesses could also be unaware or uncertain as to
how digital transformation could make their practices and transactions
better. SMEs could keep in mind rather conventional norms through
their wish to use traditional expertise, while at the same time assuming
the related prices of such practices (Kumar, M. A., and Ayedee, D. N.,
2021).
E-commerce presents additional requirements to be met by SMEs,
which very often presuppose significant modifications of established
operational processes, related organization structures, and resulting
information systems, which in general carry heavy investment needs. E-
commerce presumes a high degree of process integration and process
alignment between the SME and its external counterparts, requiring
real-time data flows and communication links, commonly utilizing the
Internet, with business and public partners extending to other countries,
most often managed in multiple languages. Whereas using the Internet’s
functionalities for occasional e-commerce they buy and sell on external
portals may be (relatively) easily established with existing business
partners, active SME participation in frequent ordering and supply chain
management processes with established business partners may instead
require the establishment of advanced electronic data interchange links
(Costa, J., Castro,R., 2021; Hussain et al., 2020; Ocloo et al., 2020).
Theorical frameworks for digital transformation
Westerman et al. (2014) proposed a foundational model in their
work "Leading Digital: Turning Technology into Business Trans­
formation," identifying two primary pillars that distinguish organiza­
tions which have successfully transformed their business and gained
competitive advantages: digital capabilities and leadership capabilities.
Digital capabilities enable organizations to leverage innovative tech­
nologies to enhance various aspects of their business. Leadership capa­
bilities, on the other hand, empower organizations to envision and steer
systematic and cost-effective organizational change. Together, these
functions enable companies to harness digital technology as a source of
competitive advantage.
In 2021, Bonnet and Westerman revised this model in their publi­
cation "The New Elements of Digital Transformation" in the MIT Sloan
Management Review, identifying three main areas of digital trans­
formation: transforming customer experience, transforming operational

In the realm of e-commerce, the externally facing element of the
framework is vital. It ensures that e-commerce platforms are not just
functional but also optimized for superior user experience, driving
higher customer satisfaction and loyalty. By integrating advanced ana­
lytics, UX design, and robust IT architecture within the e-commerce
strategy, organizations can create seamless and personalized customer
journeys. These elements help in capturing and analyzing customer data
to make informed decisions, enhance marketing strategies, and improve
overall operational efficiency.
Furthermore, the framework’s emphasis on digital platforms high­
lights the necessity of a well-structured technological backbone that
supports scalable and flexible e-commerce operations. This includes the
integration of cloud computing, big data analytics, and AI-driven solu­
tions that enable real-time responsiveness and personalization in e-
commerce environments. The leadership capabilities aspect of the
framework ensures that organizations have the vision and strategic di­
rection to implement these technologies effectively, fostering a culture
of continuous innovation and agility.
By incorporating the elements of the revised digital transformation
framework, e-commerce businesses can achieve significant improve­
ments in their digital maturity. This, in turn, can lead to enhanced
competitive positioning in the digital marketplace, better customer
retention, and increased revenue streams. The model serves as a stra­
tegic blueprint for organizations to systematically enhance their digital
capabilities and leadership, ensuring a holistic and sustainable digital
transformation.
The theoretical framework provided by Westerman et al. (2014) and
its subsequent revision by Bonnet and Westerman (2021) offers a robust
foundation for understanding and implementing digital transformation.
Its application is particularly impactful in e-commerce environments,
where the need for effective digital strategies and technologies is para­
mount to achieving business success and maintaining competitive
advantage.
There are other DT frameworks that approach TD from different
points of view. Brynjolfsson et al. (2013) propose an E-commerce
Transformation model that focuses on the transformative impact of
e-commerce on traditional business practices. Their framework high­
lights how digital platforms can significantly reduce transaction costs
and enhance market efficiency by providing better information trans­
parency and lowering barriers to entry. It underscores the importance of
network effects, where the value of a product or service increases as
more people use it and stresses the strategic use of big data to optimize
supply chain management, customer relationships, and personalization
strategies, thereby fostering a competitive advantage in the e-commerce
domain.
The Digital Maturity Model proposed by Kane et al. (2015) empha­
sizes the progression of organizations through various stages of digital
maturity, highlighting that strategic planning rather than mere tech­
nological adoption drives successful digital transformation. In the early
stage, organizations exhibit limited digital capabilities and under­
standing, often experimenting with digital tools without a cohesive
strategy. As they move into the developing stage, digital capabilities
grow, and organizations begin integrating digital tools into core oper­
ations. This stage is characterized by increasing digital investments and
a more structured approach to digital initiatives. In the maturing stage,
digital technologies are fully integrated, leading to significant business
transformation. Organizations at this stage use digital tools to enhance
customer experiences, streamline operations, and innovate business
models. Advanced stage organizations lead their industries in digital
innovation, setting new standards and continuously adapting to

Vial’s framework (2019) provides a comprehensive understanding of

Considering the existing research on digital transformation and its
implications for SMEs, the literature reviewed highlights that while
digital transformation (DT) has been extensively studied, much of the
focus remains on large enterprises. SMEs, despite their economic
importance, face unique challenges such as limited resources and
insufficient infrastructure, which hinder their digital transformation
efforts (Pelletier and Cloutier, 2019; Shehadeh, 2024). Existing frame­
works, such as those by Bonnet and Westerman (2021) and Vial (2019),
are not fully tailored to the needs of SMEs, particularly regarding
e-commerce integration, where resource efficiency and agility are crit­
ical (Verhoef et al., 2021). This review underscores the need for a
specialized framework like FASECO, which addresses the specific
organizational and technological challenges SMEs face in digital trans­
formation. By focusing on strategic alignment and data analytics, this
framework aims to enhance SMEs’ long-term competitiveness in the
digital marketplace.
Methodology
The methodology employed in this study was structured in several
comprehensive phases to evaluate the digital transformation of SMEs
and develop FASECO as a key outcome of the research.
Fig. 6 provides a visual overview of the research methodology,
summarizing the key stages from the data collection, through the

Initially, an extensive literature review was conducted to identify
best practices and challenges in the digitalization of SMEs. This analysis
included reports such as the Digital Economy and Society Index (DESI),
publications from the European Commission, and relevant case studies
on digital transformation in small and medium-sized enterprises. The
sources provided valuable data on the current state of digitalization in
Europe, common barriers faced by SMEs, and successful initiatives in
other contexts.
The DESI, developed by the European Commission, was a funda­
mental source in this phase. This index provides a detailed analysis of
the digital performance of each EU member state across five main di­
mensions: connectivity, human capital, use of internet services, inte­
gration of digital technology, and digital public services. The DESI
reports from 2020 to 2023 were used to obtain updated data on digital
progress in Europe and areas requiring priority attention. Additionally,
publications from the European Commission and other industry reports,
such as those from the Boston Consulting Group and studies by IESE-
PENTEO, were reviewed. These provided insights into the impact of
digitalization on SMEs and business leaders’ perceptions regarding
digital transformation.
Selection of interviews
In addition to the literature review, we conducted semi-structured
interviews with 13 professionals from various sectors, including tech­
nology, consulting, education, software, and advertising and marketing.
The selection of interviewees was conducted independently to ensure
objectivity and a diverse range of insights. Our sampling strategy was
purposeful, aimed at selecting participants who possess relevant
expertise and a minimum of 10 years of experience in digital trans­
formation within SMEs. Purposeful sampling is particularly appropriate
for this study because it ensures the selection of individuals with the
most relevant knowledge and experience to provide deep insights into
the challenges and opportunities of digital transformation in SMEs
(Patton, 2002). This method is crucial when seeking information-rich
cases that can address the specific research questions effectively. Addi­
tionally, semi-structured interviews were employed as they offer the
flexibility to explore participants’ perspectives in depth while main­
taining a consistent framework to address key research questions. This
approach allows for the discovery of new insights that might not emerge
from more structured methods (Bryman, 2012). Participants held key
positions such as CTOs, digital transformation consultants, IT directors,

transcribed for detailed analysis. The interview process consisted of
three parts: background information on the firm and professional,

you applied to SME e-commerce initiatives?" This combination of

theoretical insights and mitigated bias (Eisenhardt, 1989; Bingham
et al., 2007). Follow-up emails, additional interviews as needed, and
triangulation with observational and archival data were also employed
to enhance accuracy and completeness.
Data analysis
The data collected was analysed using qualitative methods enhanced
by Natural Language Processing (NLP) techniques. Thematic analysis
was employed to identify patterns and recurring themes in the expert
interviews. NLP tools, specifically keyword extraction techniques, were
used to automate the identification of key themes and patterns from the
transcripts. This approach allowed for a more structured and in-depth
understanding of the participants’ perceptions and experiences, partic­
ularly in relation to the challenges and opportunities of e-commerce
adoption in SMEs. By automating the detection of recurring patterns,
NLP tools enhanced the accuracy and depth of the thematic analysis,
providing insights that would have been difficult to capture through
manual coding alone:
•Initial Analysis: Involved within-case analysis, where each inter­
view was treated as a separate entity to understand its unique context
(Eisenhardt, 1989). This was conducted using MAXQDA software, a tool
for qualitative data analysis, which facilitated detailed coding of the
transcripts.
•Coding Process: Followed the method outlined by Miles et al.
(2014) and Miles and Huberman (1994), where data were coded at
multiple levels—words, phrases, sentences, and paragraphs—to ensure
that the contextual integrity of the information was maintained. This
comprehensive coding helped in identifying initial themes and catego­
rizing data according to predefined and emergent codes related to the
e-commerce challenges.

Results
The results of this study present the development and application of
the FASECO (Framework for Advanced Support of E-Commerce), which
aims to address the specific needs of SMEs in the context of e-commerce.
FASECO provides an advanced and holistic design for integrating e-
commerce into the digital transformation process of SMEs. This frame­
work was developed based on a comprehensive literature review,
qualitative interviews with experts, and thematic data analysis, aligning
with the revised Digital Transformation Framework by Bonnet and
Westerman (2021).
The insights gained from the interviews (Table 4), highlight several
critical aspects. For example, the importance of IT infrastructure and
advanced analytics in supporting e-commerce operations was empha­
sized by technology sector experts. Additionally, representatives from
the consulting and marketing sectors highlighted the need for a strategic
approach to online sales and customer engagement. These insights un­
derscore the multifaceted nature of digital transformation in SMEs and
the critical role that a comprehensive framework like FASECO can play
in addressing these challenges.
Bonnet and Westerman’s (2021) model identifies two fundamental
pillars in the digital transformation of organizations: digital capabilities
and leadership capabilities. Within this framework, the FASECO model
is situated at the second level of the digital platform, acting as a bridge
between the foundational technology and the data platform. FASECO
extends this structure by providing a specific focus on e-commerce,
enabling comprehensive and advanced digital customer management in
an e-commerce environment.
FASECO emerges as a response to the complex challenges of digital
transformation in e-commerce for SMEs. This model represents the main
contribution of this study, highlighting its ability to establish an inter­
face between strategy and technology, and considering the complete
customer journey, not just the specific moment of product search and
purchase. This holistic approach ensures that all necessary digital ele­
ments are accounted for, providing a concrete frame of reference to
elevate e-commerce benchmarks and enable digital leadership in SMEs.
FASECO is structured around three main dimensions (Strategy, On­
line Sales, and eCustomer Service) and four additional capabilities (User
Experience - UX, Advanced Analytics, IT Architecture, and IT Delivery &
Operation) (Fig. 7). These dimensions and capabilities cover all neces­
sary digital elements, providing a robust framework to enhance e-
commerce operations and facilitate digital transformation aligned with
strategic and technological objectives.
Strategy dimension
Digital transformation applied to e-commerce must be conceived as a
priority, impacting not only a new way of approaching the market
through an additional channel but also a global strategy that affects the
rest of the company’s strategy, processes, and applications. This stra­
tegic approach involves establishing clear objectives for the e-commerce
channel, allocating resources, and defining its role within the broader
sales and relationship channels. The study found that SMEs that consider
e-commerce as a strategic priority tend to perform better in terms of
digital engagement and customer satisfaction. This approach includes
setting specific targets for customer satisfaction, technological strategy,
and the operational model of the e-commerce platform.
Online sales dimension
Online sales are not the result of simply displaying a product in a
digital environment. They require a digital transformation process that
allows generating new ways of doing business or improving those that
are already implemented, with a contribution of value to the client that
has its origin in greater knowledge of it. The study found that SMEs that
effectively manage their online sales channels can significantly increase
their market reach and revenue streams. Key findings include the
importance of seamless and user-friendly purchase processes, person­
alized customer journeys, and the effective use of digital marketing
tools. The integration of advanced analytics helps SMEs understand
customer behavior, optimize marketing strategies, and improve con­
version rates.
eCustomer service dimension
Customer service in the digital realm extends beyond the point of
purchase. The eCustomer Service dimension ensures that customers
receive ongoing support and assistance, enhancing their overall expe­
rience and loyalty. The research highlighted the need for robust digital
customer service mechanisms, including automated support tools like
FAQs, virtual assistants, and live chat options. SMEs that invest in
comprehensive eCustomer service infrastructure tend to have higher
customer retention rates and positive brand perception.
Additional capabilities
The four additional capabilities identified—User Experience (UX),
Advanced Analytics, IT Architecture, and IT Delivery & Oper­
ation—serve as foundational elements that support the main di­
mensions. The UX capability emphasizes the importance of a user-
centric design, ensuring that e-commerce platforms are intuitive and
engaging.
Advanced analytics enable SMEs to leverage data for strategic
decision-making, providing insights into customer preferences and
market trends. IT Architecture ensures the technological backbone is
robust and scalable, supporting the evolving needs of the e-commerce
platform. Finally, IT Delivery & Operation focuses on the continuous
management and optimization of the digital infrastructure, ensuring
high availability and performance.

Discussion
The findings of this study underscore the critical importance of the
“e-commerce digitisation” indicator, which significantly contributes
(40 %) to the composition of the “Integration of Digital Technology”
index in DESI. This indicator’s relevance is further reflected in the
"Digital Spain 2025" Plan and the digital transformation plans of key
Spanish Autonomous Communities. Collectively, these plans aim to in­
crease e-commerce turnover from 11 % to 25 % by 2025. The analysis of
the 2025 digital transformation plans of Andalusia, Catalonia, Madrid,
and the Basque Country—regions that account for 57.6 % of Spain’s
GDP—reveals targeted actions to boost e-commerce.
FASECO provides a comprehensive structure for e-commerce that
surpasses existing models in several key areas. Compared to the Leading
Digital model of Westerman et al. (2014), which focuses on digital and
leadership capabilities in large organizations, FASECO is tailored spe­
cifically to the needs and constraints of SMEs. While the Westerman
model is robust, it lacks a detailed focus on e-commerce, which is critical
for SMEs’ digital transformation. FASECO fills this gap by providing
clear guidelines to enhance online sales operations, incorporating
advanced IT architecture and IT delivery capabilities that ensure SMEs
can effectively implement and scale their technological solutions. The
revised framework by Bonnet and Westerman (2021) includes elements
such as employee experience and business model innovation, yet it still
does not fully address the specific needs of e-commerce in SMEs.
FASECO surpasses this model by offering a detailed structure for
e-commerce, emphasizing customer personalization and advanced UX
tools to enhance satisfaction and retention—areas often underdeveloped
in broader digital transformation models. By focusing on these aspects,
FASECO ensures a more comprehensive approach to digital trans­
formation tailored to SMEs.
Additionally, the literature review highlights the importance of
addressing the digital divide between large enterprises and SMEs, as
noted by the European Commission (2020) and Murray (2023). These
sources emphasize the need for targeted support and frameworks that
cater specifically to the unique challenges faced by SMEs. FASECO
directly addresses these gaps by providing a tailored approach that
considers the resource constraints and specific needs of SMEs in the
e-commerce domain.
Interviews with industry professionals highlight the necessity of a
holistic approach to digital transformation. Key themes such as
advanced IT infrastructure, seamless user experience, and data-driven
decision-making align perfectly with the strategic objectives of the
FASECO framework. To process and analyse these interviews, Natural
Language Processing (NLP) techniques were employed, enhancing the
accuracy and depth of thematic analysis. NLP facilitated the extraction
of key themes and patterns from the interview transcripts, allowing for a
more nuanced understanding of the participants’ insights and
experiences.
The use of Natural Language Processing (NLP) techniques provided
not only a more efficient way of processing large volumes of interview
data, but also enabled the extraction of deeper, more nuanced insights
into organizational dynamics. These insights, which might have been
overlooked using traditional thematic analysis, revealed complex pat­
terns in how SMEs adopt e-commerce technologies, contributing to a
more comprehensive understanding of the key factors driving digital
transformation.
A critical finding of this study is the essential role of strategic inte­
gration in the digital transformation process. FASECO stresses the need
for SMEs to prioritize e-commerce within their overall business strategy.
This involves setting clear objectives, allocating resources, and defining
the role of e-commerce in the company’s sales and customer relationship
strategies.
FASECO’s holistic approach covers the entire customer journey from
strategy to post-purchase support, addressing a significant gap in current
digital transformation models. Traditional models often focus on spe­

provide a comprehensive view. By integrating UX, advanced analytics,

personalized experiences.

ensuring that SMEs can leverage these strategies to enhance their
competitiveness in the digital marketplace (Brynjolfsson et al., 2013).
The Digital Maturity Model proposed by Kane et al. emphasizes the
progression of organizations through various stages of digital maturity,
highlighting that strategic planning rather than mere technological
adoption drives successful digital transformation. Kane et al. emphasize
that organizations must focus on developing a digital strategy aligned
with their business objectives, fostering a culture of continuous learning
and adaptation to achieve digital maturity. FASECO complements this
model by providing a structured approach to integrating e-commerce
within the broader digital transformation strategy, ensuring that SMEs
can progress through these stages effectively (Kane et al., 2015).
Vial’s framework provides a comprehensive understanding of digital
transformation by focusing on the interplay between technological ad­
vancements and organizational change. This model highlights that
digital transformation is not just about adopting new technologies but
involves a significant shift in organizational processes, culture, and
value creation mechanisms. Aligning digital strategies with organiza­
tional goals and fostering a culture of continuous learning and adapt­
ability are crucial elements of this framework. FASECO aligns with
Vial’s principles by emphasizing the need for strategic integration,
continuous innovation, and a customer-centric approach to digital
transformation, particularly in the context of e-commerce (Vial, 2019).
Conclusion
The discussion highlights the strengths and relevance of the FASECO
framework for digital transformation in SMEs. By comparing it with
existing models, such as those proposed by Westerman et al. (2014),
Bonnet and Westerman (2021), Brynjolfsson et al. (2013), Kane et al.
(2015), and Vial (2019), it is evident that FASECO offers a tailored and
comprehensive approach to e-commerce integration. Its holistic nature
ensures that SMEs can navigate the complexities of digital trans­
formation effectively, leveraging advanced technologies and strategic
planning to enhance their competitiveness in the digital marketplace.
The FASECO framework, complemented by the innovative use of
Natural Language Processing (NLP) techniques in the analysis of quali­
tative data, offers a unique contribution to the understanding of e-
commerce adoption in SMEs. The deeper insights gained through this
approach reveal organizational patterns and challenges that traditional
methods may overlook, further emphasizing the importance of holistic
frameworks that integrate both technological and organizational factors
in digital transformation processes.
Despite the strengths of the FASECO model, the study has limitations
that suggest areas for future research. The model is particularly suitable
for SMEs with 10 or more employees, where the volume and complexity
of customer relationships can benefit from its application. However, for
micro-SMEs, the model’s applicability may vary due to company size,
possibly necessitating partial application. Future research should
explore adapting the model for very small companies and identifying
which dimension has the least impact on e-commerce turnover. Addi­
tionally, extending this model to collaborative environments could
facilitate data integration and sharing between companies, promoting
collaborative economies and revitalizing post-COVID sectors. Different
levels of model simplification could be evaluated to suit diverse SME
profiles, providing a basis for building e-commerce management skill
banks to help SMEs navigate digital transformation successfully.
There are also potential improvements regarding the surveys con­
ducted. Although the semi-structured interviews provided valuable in­
sights, expanding the sample size and including a more diverse range of
industries and regions could enhance the robustness of the findings.
Furthermore, incorporating quantitative surveys alongside qualitative
interviews might offer a more comprehensive understanding of the
digital transformation landscape in SMEs. This mixed-method approach
could validate the qualitative insights and provide statistical signifi­
cance to the findings.
Further research should also focus on validating the FASECO model
by exploring the integration of emerging technologies such as artificial
intelligence, machine learning, and blockchain within the FASECO
framework. This could provide new insights and enhance its
applicability.
The integration of open innovation practices, particularly through
external collaborations and shared technological solutions, presents an
additional strategy that SMEs can adopt to enhance their digital trans­
formation efforts. By leveraging external knowledge and resources,
SMEs can overcome their resource limitations and access new markets,
thereby accelerating their digitalization journey and fostering greater
competitiveness.
In addition to the avenues for future research, the findings of this
study also provide actionable recommendations for practitioners and
policymakers. For practitioners, particularly SME managers, it is crucial
to prioritize the alignment between technological adoption and orga­
nizational processes. This alignment ensures that e-commerce solutions
not only fit the specific needs of the business but also contribute to long-
term competitiveness. Moreover, the integration of data-driven deci­
sion-making tools, such as those enhanced by Natural Language Pro­
cessing (NLP), can provide deeper insights into customer behavior and
market trends, supporting more informed and strategic decisions.
Policymakers, in turn, can foster the digital transformation of SMEs
by creating supportive frameworks that provide access to affordable
digital tools and training programs. Offering incentives for the adoption
of e-commerce technologies would further strengthen the digital
competitiveness of SMEs, enabling them to navigate the challenges of
the digital marketplace with greater agility and success.
