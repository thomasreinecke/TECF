Abstract
Smart Cities are complex systems where digital innovations are applied to design enhanced city services and enable public value
creation across various domains (e.g., mobility, energy and infrastructure, environment, and so). Such complexity challenges the
alignment between city strategies with city services and underlying information systems, leading to providing services that may
fail to achieve city goals and meet the needs of citizens. This paper defines a set of design principles to ensure strategic alignment
in Smart City Enterprise Architectures (SCEA). The design principles were derived from the literature and validated by smart
city domain experts, following a design-oriented research approach. The main contribution of this paper is the formulated design
principles that deepen the knowledge of Enterprise Architecture design in the smart cities field. These design principles can be
used by researchers and practitioners to guide the design of innovative digital services aligned with urban strategies.

solutions that serve the needs of the citizens and urban environments [27, 1, 8]. Many municipalities have to face
challenges in the development and implementation of smarter cities. They need to move from a traditional model of
service delivery to a more citizen-centric approach, contributing to the success of Smart City initiatives [29].
City services and underlying information systems must support a continuous strategic alignment during digital
transformation. The effective transition of strategy into IT requires extensive design activity on both organisation
design and information systems design [21]. [19] present a Strategic Alignment Model (SAM) which defines the
different perspectives for the integration between business and IT. In this model, business strategy drives both the
organisational design choices and the design of IS, while IT enables new or enhanced business strategies. Several
analytical methodologies are available to make the strategic alignment operational: critical success factors, IS business
systems planning, Enterprise Architecture (EA), and Enterprise Modelling (EM) [19, 12, 37]. Unlike the existing
research that considers strategic alignment in the corporate and profit-oriented sector, strategic alignment in the Smart
Cities domain is still an open challenge [40]. Smart Cities are required to achieve city goals and objectives and provide
the desired services to citizens, with challenging implications in the management and design of services [15]. Citizen
centricity must influence the design and delivery of city services driven by the needs of citizens (e.g., residents,
visitors, and businesses) [23].
Smart Cities Enterprise Architectures (SCEA) aim to provide a common framework for stakeholders and a guide
to address their multiple concerns [4, 9]. Several SCEA are proposed to face the challenges of digitalising and trans-
forming public services [27, 33, 16, 31]. Yet, these architectures mostly emphasize technical aspects that constitute
Smart Cities, and they rarely focus on city services and their strategic aspects towards delivering cities’ vision and ob-
jectives [35, 40]. Smart Cities are likely to fail to offer the required services to citizens because of this lack of strategic
alignment considerations that negatively impact the achievement of city goals and objectives [32]. Design principles
are necessary as general design considerations to address the alignment in the Smart Cities domain. Hence, this paper
answers the following research question (RQ): “What are the design principles that support the strategic alignment
between city services and information systems in SCEA?”. Such design principles are formulated as explicit prescrip-
tions to answer this research question and facilitate design-science research on modelling the strategic alignment in
SCEA. The main contribution of this paper is the formulation of a set of comprehensive design principles that deepen
the knowledge of architectural design in the Smart Cities field. These principles can be used by researchers and prac-
titioners to develop innovative digital services aligned with urban strategies. The structure of the paper is organised as
follows, Section 1 introduces and motivates the research problem. Section 2 details the research method and Section 3
presents the set of design principles. Section 4 discusses the formulated principles and concludes the paper.
2. Research Method
This paper defines a set of design principles to ensure strategic alignment between city services and information
systems in SCEA. The design principles were derived from relevant literature and validated by Smart City domain
experts as part of a design science research process [7]. The literature review process and the case studies are detailed
as follows.
2.1. Literature Review
The search strategy follows a structured approach for literature reviews in order to determine the source material
for the review [44]. The initial set of papers is selected by retrieving all the titles of the papers published by a relevant
set of scientific journals and conferences on topics regarding Information Systems, Enterprise Architecture, and Smart
Cities. We searched for literature in a database consisting of the “AIS Basket of 8” and open access databases such
as Google Scholar. The keywords used in the search process include: smart city alignment, enterprise architecture
alignment, alignment principle(s), and alignment design. The keywords are searched in the title, abstract, and key-
words terms. A total of thirty-five journal articles and twenty-seven conference articles were identified. These papers
are then selected and reviewed entirely. Next, the first set of design principles was identified by reflecting on the
existing knowledge for alignment in EA and design knowledge acquired from working on modelling public services.
Successively, additional relevant papers are identified proceeding backward by reviewing the citations of the identified

2.2. Case Studies
This paper validates the design principles identified by conducting multiple case studies on two real-world cities
in Ireland and Israel. The two cities will be referred to as River City and Riviera City. These case studies rely on
multiple sources of evidence (e.g., meetings, semi-structured interviews, internal documents, digital strategies), using
a triangulation analysis. The data of the case studies are collected by using a direct method and independent analy-
sis [38]. The stakeholders in River city include the head of digital strategy, the data manager, and the senior managers
of the city council departments involved in the Smart City initiative. The stakeholders in Riviera city include the Smart
City and digital domain manager, Geographic Information System (GIS) manager, and a process owner. We followed
an inductive and iterative process of reading and examining the transcriptions and the secondary data to assign the
information analysis units to the coding concepts related to the dimensions and design principles. These dimensions
and principles were refined after three iteration loops with the stakeholders as presented in the next section.

serve the needs of citizens [27, 1, 42]. The needs of citizens involve the needs of individuals, but also the needs of
communities, groups, neighbourhoods, and businesses of the city [14, 23, 2]. Smart cities are required to develop new
ways of working across vertical silos to deliver more citizen-centric services, contributing to the success of Smart City

4. Discussion and Conclusion
Strategic alignment is required in the Smart Cities field in order to respond to city goals across various domains
(e.g., mobility, energy, environment, and public safety). This research builds an understanding of the different design
principles that can guide the design of SCEA. Hence, four dimensions (e.g., objective, stage, scope, mechanism)
and six distinct design principles (e.g., citizen’s needs focus, consistency, compatibility, dynamicity, vertical and
horizontal, modelling) are identified and validated with Smart City domain experts of cities. These design principles
were defined as statements that describe how the alignment should be designed or built or what it should comprise.
Our findings deepen the understanding of the strategic alignment in SCEA and add design principles based on our
work with Smart City domain experts in two different cities. This study advances current research on the Smart
Cities field, which has been mainly focused on the implementation of the information and technology aspects in
these complex and dynamic urban environments. It contributes to the current understanding of how city strategies
should be aligned with Smart City implementations. Besides, this research provides a prescriptive view to guide a
coherent architectural design to support the fulfilment of the Smart Cities vision. Such a prescriptive view ensures
that Smart City implementations are built according to city goals which reflect the needs and expectations of citizens.
Our case studies validate this prescriptive view by analysing these design principles in line with city master plans and
digital strategies. This is important to advance the concept of Smart Cities, as research has so far primarily focused
on technical and engineering challenges with little attention to how to achieve city strategies [35, 40]. It is necessary
to answer during the city service design, why these services exist or why they need to be changed. This can help city
administrators and architects develop coherent architecture descriptions to support continuous alignment [25].
The formulated design principles can be used as a foundation for design science research that seek to develop
new or improved SCEA aligned with city strategies. These design principles provide a solid foundation to break the
existing silos and ensure the interoperability of Smart Cities at various levels (e.g., strategic, city services, information
systems). Our research work leads us to understand that city managers should ensure alignment with city strategies,
tracing city services and information systems. Future research will investigate the strategic alignment by using a
semantic analysis of the SCEA models. This will allow the design of integrated SCEA to reduce the silos that currently
exist across public services.
